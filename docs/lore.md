- **The Destroyer and White Bird were rival suitors of Star Mother.** She refused both: the

### Worship and idols
Temples are like the priest's house. Idols stand around the island, and **each kind of idol is a
gameplay location**:

| Idol | Where | In the game |
|------|-------|-------------|
| The Red Bowl | Everywhere | **Save points** |
| Star Mother | Healers' houses, hot springs | Healing and rest |
| The Star Child | Graveyards | (with the dead) |
| The Bird | Fast travel points, telegraph stations | **Fast travel** |

**The Lady of the Sun's chaining:** Islanders believe she was **dangerous**, so they see Juda's
chaining of her as protection.

### How the All-Seafarers see the gods
A **henotheistic** position: the (male, in their view) **Red Bowl is the true god and creator**, and
**His children and their descendants are an untrustworthy, dangerous but still divine squabbling
family of incestuous thieves and criminals, who had to be tamed for humanity's benefit**. Except, of
course, the King of All the Sea. **Half the reason they are invading is that they know the children
of the Star Mother (the Ladies) live on the island.**

### The founding of the Kingdom of All the Sea
About **65 years before the game**, **the King**, **Juda** and **the Lady of the Mountains** went on a
classic JRPG adventure: righting wrongs, bringing various Ladies and their husbands into humanity's
service or sealing them away, and founding the Kingdom of All the Sea.

**The Lady of the Mountains** lived on the crater rim, split into two parts: **the Dark Lady** of the
rainy, fertile side of the rim, and **the Red Lady** of the endless, trackless jagged highlands
beyond. She became **the King's wife (wives)**: she split herself permanently into two beings and
**became mortal**, so their very many children (twins, triplets and more, from both bodies) are
**unusually powerful humans**. Theenmy and the Princess are among them. It was **a love match**, and
also her escape from her divine family's drama. The Dark Lady and the Red Lady are **alive and live in
the capital**.

**Some Ladies fight for the Princess**: Ladies that the founding party conscripted into the empire's
service.

### The island is the center of the world
**The Smoking Isle sits at the center of the world-crater**: the central peak where the stolen star
came down.

### Juda's identities
1. **A Navigator Lord of the First Sailors**, millennia old, his soul in a moai's coral eyes.
2. **The "old wizard" of the King's party** (65 years ago), a Gandalf figure. He simulated aging with
   magic and **"died" about 50 years ago**.
3. **A wandering mystic in the form of a teenager**, who came to the island and was **chosen as
   apprentice by the previous Islander priest**.
4. **The village priest:** he simulates aging with magic, and the Islanders think he is a very hale
   man in his late sixties. (His "orphan" backstory belongs to this identity.)

**Neither the King nor Theenmy knows** that the island's priest is the King's old companion. **The
previous priest, who chose Juda as apprentice, was Juda's first murder on the island.**

### The Red Bowl
Genderless god of the seas and the world, and of sea life: the whole world and all its seas,
personified. Idols are carved bowls of red scoria filled with pure water. **These idols are the
save points**, so the player interacts with the Red Bowl more than any other god. Not
anthropomorphic: the world itself as a divine entity.

- **Imperial heresy:** the All-Seafarers insist the Red Bowl is male and specifically the father of
  their King of All the Sea. This is imperial theology reshaping a universal, genderless deity to
  legitimize a dynasty. Every island that worships the Red Bowl as genderless commits quiet heresy
  against imperial doctrine.
- **Third gender priesthood:** the Red Bowl's genderlessness is one foundation of the island's
  third gender priestly role. If the divine is genderless, its mediators should be too.
- **Origin memory:** the Red Bowl may be a mythological memory of the world's true origin, an impact
  crater filled with water. The idol is a scale model of what literally happened; red, perhaps,
  from the violence of its creation.

### Star Mother
Goddess of the stars, especially the polar star: the fixed point everything else turns around.
The constant, the grieving mother, the one who endures. Her child, the Star Child, was killed by
the White Bird. One of her stars was stolen by the Destroyer to make the volcano, so part of her
domain was taken and turned into fire and earth.

### The Star Child
The Star Mother's eldest child, believed to be **the first human**, and genderless in island
cosmology. Killed by the White Bird, but as a god could not truly die: their body fell to earth as
shooting stars and became **the First Sailors, and thus all humans**.

The theological mirror of Yoon's story: a child killed, whose death was generative, whose body
became something new. The game's central narrative retold at cosmic scale. Also, alongside the Red
Bowl, a foundation of the third gender priesthood: the first human had no gender.

### White Bird / Sooty Bird
Creator of humanity and fertility god. Killed the Star Child in a dispute, and so is both a murderer
and a creator, and remorseful. To redeem himself he stole the stolen star back from the Destroyer,
but dropped it too low in the sky. It burned him, turning White Bird into **Sooty Bird**, blackened
and scarred. The star became **the Sun**. The rock he tore from the island while pulling the star
free became **the Moon**, which he took as his home: the islanders' man in the moon.

**Juda's mirror in the sky:** violence, then guilt, then an attempt to fix things that creates
further consequences, leaving him permanently transformed. The parallel to a man who murdered a
child, cried during the ritual, and stayed trapped by the consequences.

### The Destroyer
A malevolent deity who in ancient times stole one of the Star Mother's stars and took it to the
mountain. Its heat melted the mountain into **the volcano**, where he supposedly lives at the
center. The volcano, the lava tubes and the volcanic desert are his domain; his servants (Black
Hounds, Hunched) emerge from its depths.

May be connected to the First Sailors' demon-summoning. The lava tubes being both his domain and
burial sites suggests a link between death, demons and the volcanic underworld. **Yoon waking in a
lava tube over a skeleton is waking in the Destroyer's house.**

### The Ladies
Minor goddesses and powerful spirits of the island's natural features, children of the Star Mother
and the Star Child. Not part of the high pantheon: too powerful and ancient to be human, too local
and personal to be cosmic gods. The islanders treat them as neighbors. Each has her own domain,
court and temperament: the island's spiritual infrastructure, the divine made local.

Each took a mortal husband over the centuries, who eventually became minor gods by adoption:

| Lady | Domain | Husband(s) |
|------|--------|------------|
| Lady of the Woods | The forests | The Master of Deception, the Shepherd of the Deer |
| Lady of the Wind | Marsh, coasts, mountain peak | The Bull of Water |
| Lady of the Sun | Once the desert (now chained) | The Bull of Heaven |

## 2. The Forest

### The Lady of the Woods
Lives in **two bodies in two places at once**, with two aspects:
- **Temperate rainforest:** a woman with a porcine aspect; temperamental but gregarious.
- **Alpine forest:** a great goat-centaur; polite but cold.

Commands the People of the Forest. Has been cultivating a special herb under every blue moon for
four hundred years to grow the Familiar (Falmi). Her three daughters became trapped in First Sailor
wells and were transformed into giant snake women.

Rules the forest through **negotiated agreements** with the islanders: fallen-wood limits, fire
clearings, marked trees, hunting quotas. Enforced by the Owls of Ill Omen and the boar-men.

*In the game:* quest-giver, potential ally, and an authority to be negotiated with. Permission to
hunt the Great Boar requires a major favor, such as freeing one of her Daughters. During the
invasion her domain turns hostile to invaders who break the forest agreements.

### The Master of Deception
One of the Lady's husbands; shapeshifter of the temperate rainforest. Can appear as a crying child,
a beautiful young woman or a handsome man. True form: shaggy goat legs ending in hooves, hairy
torso, half-human half-goat head with yellow rectangular-pupilled eyes, horns under shaggy hair.

Considers himself the protector of the woods and will lead the boar-men into battle at need. A
mischievous prankster who stops short of serious harm unless the target genuinely threatens the
forest. **Cannot dream** and is fascinated by others' dreams: **islander priests learn from him in
exchange for offering their dreams.** Sib likes him and calls him **"Uncle Deception."**

*In the game:* friendly NPC, teacher, trickster, potential wartime ally (his shapeshifting could be
devastating against invader patrols). His relationship with Sib is the foundation of the wartime
alliance with the forest.

### The Shepherd of the Deer
The Lady's other husband; guardian of the alpine forest. About six feet tall, muscular upper body,
boar-like lower limbs, face hidden by coarse bristles except fiery round red eyes, twin-tipped tail,
a gnarled shepherd's crook as tool and weapon.

Protects wild animals and the balance between predator and prey: warns animals of traps and
hunters, leads them to safety, disorients pursuers. Hunters lose their way; their prey vanishes.
**Islander priests undergo long quests to find him and teach him new songs in exchange for stealth
magic.**

*In the game:* neutral NPC, ally or obstacle depending on the player's relationship with the
forest. A complicated relationship with Pada the hunter. Source of stealth magic through priestly
questlines.

### The Familiar
See Falmi in concept.md: a small elf-like child grown from the Lady's herb.

### Daughters of the Wells
The Lady's three daughters, trapped in First Sailor wells and transformed into giant snake women
who try to pull people down to their deaths. If "killed" they revert to their true forms: beautiful
water maidens of the woodland rivers.

*In the game:* bosses that are really **rescue missions**. Freeing one may be required for
permission to hunt the Great Boar. They introduce the player to **First Sailor binding magic**
before the chains Juda used on the Bull of Heaven.

### Boar-Men (People of the Forest, male)
Swine-men, sometimes bipedal, sometimes on all fours; skilled warriors with rudimentary spears or
clubs. Originally ordinary boars ensorcelled by the Lady. One matches five men in strength. Two
tribes: temperate lowland and alpine highland.

Blunt, not bright, but **negotiable**: they respond to directness and honesty, and **flattery,
deception and clever wordplay actively backfire**. They enforce the forest agreements.

*In the game:* enemies if forest rules are broken or boundaries crossed, negotiable through Talk.
During the invasion, effective guerrilla fighters against invaders who don't know the agreements.

### Stream Maidens (People of the Forest, female)
Intelligent, beautiful maidens with goat legs who live by the forest's streams, converse with
spirits and know nature deeply. The Lady's messengers and handmaidens; probably mark which trees
may be cut. Two tribes: the slow river of the temperate forest and the fast mountain streams of the
alpine forest.

*In the game:* friendly NPCs offering information (plants, water safety, animal movements) and
sidequest hooks. Sources of botanical and ecological knowledge.

### Forest Child-Fae (People of the Forest, children)
Small fairies: boys with porcine faces, girls with hooves; parents often too busy to raise them.
Capable of impish malevolence (frightening children, pranks, following and mocking people lost in
the woods), but harmless: they feel bad and lead you out if you start to cry. They can **dissolve
into a pool of water** when frightened or captured. They ride nearly invisible **Green Horses**
made of leaves and boughs.

*In the game:* ambient NPCs, minor hazards and comic relief that make the forest a living
community. **A warning system: if child-fae start mocking you, you've crossed a boundary.**

### Owls of Ill Omen
Dire owls with long saber-shaped beaks used to suck blood and pupil-less amber eyes. They gather on
ruined First Sailor towers, and are given leave to attack those who harm the forests.

*In the game:* enemies triggered by serious violations (cutting unmarked trees, fires outside
clearings, entering sacred groves): the escalation between warnings and a full boar-man response.
**Tou's presence may stop them attacking**, through residual First Sailor command hierarchy.

### The Great Boar
The largest and most significant animal in the Lady's forest, possibly known to her personally and
possibly protected by the Shepherd. Hunting it without permission brings the forest's full
enforcement down on the hunter.

*In the game:* the **Act 2** climactic boss. Killing it provides **the
flesh ingredient for Yoon's homunculus body**. Requires the Lady's permission, which requires a
major favor. **The showcase for animal Talk**: tracking, reading signs, choosing the right approach.

## 3. Other Spirits and Peoples

### Children of the Caves
- **Females:** beautiful small winged women in red, with silver-embroidered hats; can produce
  blinding light.
- **Males:** short and chubby, in long vestments and a red broad-brimmed hat over curly hair.

Attracted to shiny objects, which they hide in secret deposits. Experts in underground passages.
Mostly harmless pranksters: pulling clothes off people, stealing quilts, harassing domestic animals,
especially sheep. Normally visible only to children and the innocent. **By islander tradition each
newborn is assigned one as a guardian angel.** They appear in the dead of night to people in
desperate need after all human aid has failed, and lead them to hidden treasure with no
conditions, promises or repayment.

**Secret:** they descend from **discarded infant and child homunculi produced by the First Sailors'
child-sacrifice industry**. They have a hidden village in the lava tubes and are mostly unaware of
their origins. Digging, they eventually discover Queen Tou in her buried palace and "serve" her.

*In the game:* friendly NPCs. **Lasa** guides Yoon out of the caves in the prologue (Act 1), helps
gather body ingredients in Act 2 and stays as a **permanent party member** from then on, and
plays a key role in the Tou questline.

### Queen Tou
The last First Sailor monarch: a **nine-year-old girl sacrificed by her parents' court**, her soul
put into an articulated wooden doll as a puppet queen. Fed false information about the outside
world for centuries, she unknowingly signed death warrants for children she believed she was
fostering. Buried by a volcanic avalanche, she slept for thousands of years until bored Children
of the Caves dug her up. Immortal through her **moai phylactery**: the doll re-forms after two turns.

### Stonemen of the Desert
Ancient statues quarried in the volcanic desert but never moved to their intended sites. They come
to life as large, brutish beings with hard dusky stone skin, a very simple tribal structure, little
clothing and large clubs. **Leaders wear red scoria headdresses, speak broken First Sailor tongue,
and wield fire magic and magic that turns people into salt.** They guard First Sailor ruins.

Made of **tuff, the softest stone on the island**, a subtle hint at their vulnerability. Residual
magic in the ruins gave them crude sentience. They may still serve masters present in the moai, coral
eyes watching from inside carved stone.

*In the game:* among the toughest regular enemies, in the desert and the ruins. **They surrender
immediately to Tou**, their queen; the chain of command reactivates.

### Ghosts
Many varieties. The dead are neighbors: islanders treat ghosts like any other community member.
**Ghosts often believe Yoon is a ghost too**, so Yoon can often speak with them peaceably.
- *Example:* the spirit of a woman who died in childbirth, seen along rivers between one and three in
  the morning, washing bloody clothes and singing a sad lullaby.
- **First Sailor ghosts** in the deepest lava tubes speak a language only Tou understands, and have
  drifted purposelessly for millennia. Tou gives them someone to report to, turning dangerous
  encounters into reunions.

### Hags of the Salt Marsh
Ancient First Sailor women whose dead bodies are possessed by weaker demons. They serve the Lady of
the Wind. Vampiric witches who feed on marsh animals and sometimes interlopers; ugly old hags with
stumpy legs who can turn into beautiful marsh cranes. Not wholly hostile: **they treat Yoon like
cooing grandmothers.** That affection is genuine demonic familiarity: they recognize something
kindred in Yoon, dressed in the social habits of the dead women they inhabit.

*In the game:* negotiable enemies with dialogue advantages for Yoon. **Tou creates a complicated
reaction:** the bodies may recognize their queen, conflicting with the demons driving them. The
Lady of the Wind's authority offers another avenue.

### The Lady of the Wind
A strict, beautiful woman who fluctuates in the wind. Angry, as a storm, she scratches the faces of
those who have sinned and uproots trees. Protects the marsh, the coasts and the mountain peak.
*In the game:* quest-giver, potential boss, authority over the open, exposed parts of the island,
paralleling the Lady of the Woods.

### The Bull of Water
The Lady of the Wind's mortal husband, transformed into a **blue minotaur**. He committed terrible
murders, and since an immortal couldn't be killed, she chained him in the sea. **The All-Seafarers
believe his lie that he is the Bull of Heaven and release him**, to their dismay. Very strong, with a
keen mind and no sense of law or loyalty; uses every means to coerce and destroy; **can hypnotize**.
*In the game:* a major threat released by imperial ignorance (their theology assumed their myths
map onto island reality). Dangerous because he is intelligent and persuasive.

### The Lady of the Sun
A beautiful woman of gigantic proportions who appears in summer. Very arrogant and cruel. Drags
heavy chains and lives in a grotto at the foot of the volcano. Seeing her means impending death.
Once ruled the desert. **Chained by Juda with First Sailor chain technology, which is how he became
head priest.**
*In the game:* a chained NPC who can be freed, **a major moral choice**: wrongfully imprisoned, but
genuinely arrogant and cruel. Tou's presence may change things, since the Lady would recognize First
Sailor royalty.

### The Bull of Heaven
The Lady of the Sun's mortal husband, transformed into a **white minotaur with fires burning on his
horns**. On full moon nights he bellows outside the house of anyone who has committed a serious
crime, specifically **Juda's house**. Imprisoned with his wife by Juda, who deciphered First Sailor
chain spells to neutralize divine punishment: **Juda silenced his own accuser.**
*In the game:* absent at first; once freed, a divine justice figure. His bellowing outside Juda's
house is a mystery clue: if the Bull of Heaven marks your door, what did you do?

### Servants of the Destroyer
- **Black Hounds:** demonic wolf-like beasts with shaggy black fur and glowing red eyes, born from
  minor demons possessing the corpses of rabid dogs. They live in deep mountain caves and emerge at
  night to drag livestock and people into the underworld. **Cannot be negotiated with.** Patience
  being a dog makes these encounters a narrative beat.
- **Hunched:** zombie-like corpses used as puppets, with deaths-head faces of unending pain and long
  grasping claws. They kill livestock in darkness and raid during storms. They kidnap people and drag
  them before the Destroyer, who forces a choice: submit and make a deal, or have your soul
  devoured. They wail and keen in combat, overcome with sorrow; the player isn't defeating evil but
  destroying enslaved corpses begging for release. **Talk should allow trying to free them.**

### Other beings
- **Guardian Lizards:** giant gecko or lizard spirits guarding pools and waterways (the crater lake,
  springs, the river's source). Territorial; require a proper ritual approach (Sib's advantages
  apply); powerful bosses if provoked, peaceful if respected.
- **Giant Clams:** monster clams of the deep; underwater threats once Dama's breathing spell opens
  the sea. Hazards as much as enemies.
- **Dream Hunters:** living people who wander the night in trance, meeting spirits of the dead. If
  they see a living person's spirit, that person will die. One in the village **foresaw Pada's death
  but couldn't prevent it**.
- **Sleep Demons:** sit on your chest during nightmares; warded off by verbal rituals known to a wise
  woman, passed from mother to daughter. Attack through dreams; potentially a unique non-combat
  encounter type.
- **Navigator-Lord remnants:** uncertain.

## 3a. The Islanders

### Origins
The Islanders **arrived several centuries after the First Sailors were gone** and found the island
abandoned. They know the moai and ruins were built by people.

**All-Seafarer prejudices (all untrue):**
1. Tattooing is a primitive practice descended from First Sailor slave marks.
2. The Islanders descend from First Sailor slaves, which is why they are "pitiable" and primitive.
3. Islanders "sleep with" wild animals, and that is where the People of the Forest come from.

### Society
An informal, fairly democratic chiefdom (a "big man" society) with an unusual amount of customary
bureaucracy for that level. **One main town** (Zonfahare), larger satellite towns with their own
priests, and small resource villages. See "Settlements and networks" below.

### Government
A nebulous middle ground between a big-man society and a "democratic" chiefdom: customary and
kinship-based at heart, with an odd amount of bureaucracy for its size (it runs on printing and wire).
Real models: Melanesian big men, the Guanche *tagoror* council circle, Rapa Nui clans and scribes, and
Iceland's assembly and lawspeaker.

- **Households and kin lines** settle most things: marriages, land, feuds, debts. Disputes end in
  **compensation** between kin lines, with a priest as go-between; **exile** is the worst punishment.
- **Big People:** each town has a Big Man or Big Woman (Dama in Mansehare). Not hereditary: standing
  comes from **giving** (feasts, sponsoring boats, covering debts, organising hunts and harvests), and
  people follow by choice. A big kin line helps.
- **The Island Chief** is the Big Person the others recognise as first among equals at **the Assembly**:
  a seasonal island-wide meeting at a **stone circle in Zonfahare**. Any head of a household may speak;
  decisions are by consensus. The Chief mostly persuades, settles disputes between towns, and speaks for
  the island to outsiders. **Towns that can't send anyone follow the Assembly by telegraph**: speeches
  are read out at the far relay and answers tapped back.
- **The odd bureaucracy:**
  - **The Printed Custom:** after each Assembly the year's decisions and customs are printed and sent to
    every town. A **Keeper of Custom** (lawspeaker) remembers, recites and rules on custom.
  - **Telegraph keepers** staff every relay and **log every message** (mystery evidence).
  - **Rotating duties** (*keu*, "communal enterprise"): households take turns at line repair, bothy
    upkeep, battery tending and water rights.
  - **Priests as registrars:** births, deaths, mummifications, omens, the **register of the dead**. This
    gives Juda quiet power over what the village believes about deaths and missing children.
  - **Tally houses:** cowrie accounts, the orchil trade, and the **tribute ledger**.

**Tama, the Island Chief.** Likable, gregarious and smart, but a bit spineless. He goes to **Juda** for
advice on many things, and he's the main advocate of **paying the All-Seafarers' tribute** and keeping the
peace with them. The island has paid tribute for **about 30 years, on Juda's advice** (part of Juda's
collaboration). When the invasion comes, **Theenmy keeps Tama around as a puppet** chief; the Islanders
follow whoever actually provides and protects, which is how **Dama becomes Chieftess by default**.

**Why it matters in the story:** the All-Seafarers expect a king to deal with or behead; the Islanders
have a consensus web held together by kin, giving and wire, so a puppet chief doesn't buy obedience.

### Priests
Priests heal, hold funerals, **mummify the dead**, talk to ghosts and spirits, and read omens. They
are chosen from the children Islander culture already counts as **priests or potential priests**:
**twins**, **orphans** (often raised by priests), **intersex and third gender** children, and those who
have metaphorically **"died and come back"** (mostly children revived after drowning). **Juda's (false)
backstory is that he was an orphan.**

### Death
The dead are mummified and laid in **stone-cut tombs in the lava tubes**. A ghost is not alarming
in itself: ghost Yoon is *concerning* because Yoon was a child who went missing about a month before
the game starts, and because Yoon has no memories. **Juda defleshed Yoon's body**, which is why Yoon wakes over a bare skeleton only a month after
going missing. **The ritual Sib uses to build Yoon a body is
forbidden**: Sib learned it from **notes Juda made about First Sailor homunculus production**.

### Seeing the dead
**Only priests and potential priests can see ghosts without help:** twins, orphans, intersex and third
gender people, and those who "died and came back". Belief is magic on the island: because the culture
counts them as priests, they can see the dead. Ordinary people, adults and children alike, cannot.
(Children see the Children of the Caves, not ghosts.) **Sib** (intersex, third gender, and a priest's
apprentice) and **Yoon's twin siblings** can all see ghost Yoon.

**Yoon counts as a priest.** Yoon literally died and came back, the purest case of what drowning
survivors only do metaphorically (hence the class name, **Returned Child**). That is why Yoon can bear a
**Face tattoo** (priests only), use priestly gear (jasper weapons, the Feather set, staves) and read
spirits through Insight. This is why Act 1 is spent struggling to make
contact, and why Sib, a priest's apprentice, is the one Yoon reaches. It also means **Juda can see
ghost Yoon**.

### Tattoos and glyphs
Tattoos are **glyphs of the same script the First Sailors wrote in**, and carry a magical
**true-name** power. They were given to humankind by **Sooty Tern as an apology gift**; a myth tells
how he based the first glyphs on the **crack patterns in eggshells**. Some glyphs are rumored to be
**the Destroyer's distortion of the system**, which the First Sailors used. (So the All-Seafarer
"slave marks" slur mocks a sacred script they cannot read.)

**Where tattoos go:** the lower and upper arms, and **the face, which only priests may tattoo**. Yoon's
face carries a glyph nobody can read, which holds their ghost abilities: **the name of the
Destroyer** (revealed later).

### Weapons and metal
- Islanders make **copper spears, axes, maces and long knives**, but **not swords proper**. Their only
  "swords" are fire-hardened two-handed swords of native **dragonheart wood**, and obsidian-edged
  wooden swords (macuahuitl-style).
- **Silver matters spiritually:** copper-silver weapons are slightly more durable and add a little
  magic damage to every attack.
- **Conducting Edge is an Islander technique.**
- **Cattle:** domesticated cattle came with the All-Seafarers; the island has rare wild,
  aurochs-like cattle.
- **Horses:** the island has none, and Islanders have **never seen one** until the invasion (Act 4),
  when the All-Seafarers bring **horses and chariots**. Chariots need open, flat ground (grassland,
  the Zonfahare ridge, Afarohare's terraces) and are useless in forest, tubes, marsh or mountains.

### Technology: electricity without bronze
Despite having no bronze, the Islanders have **electricity, telegraphs and printing**.

- **Batteries:** stacks of alternating copper and lead discs soaked in acidic water from volcanic hot
  springs and fumaroles, each stack in a ceramic jar, several jars wired together. **Battery
  tenders** maintain them, replacing spent discs and refreshing the acid.
- **Uses:** electroplating ritual objects and jewelry in silver; brine electrolysis for lye (soap,
  disinfectant); short-circuited wire to start fires on command; mild current through copper probes
  for medical and ceremonial purposes. Also lighting, electromagnets, electrochemical refining,
  **electric cautery and surgery**, electro-etching, hydrogen and oxygen, **electric fences**,
  chlorine, and **crude seismographs**.
- **Telegraph:** copper-silver alloy wire insulated with rubber (latex from Eucommia-type trees,
  cured with volcanic sulfur). A key operator taps a hinged copper switch; a receiver watches a
  lodestone needle twitch inside a copper coil. Short and long twitches form a binary code.
- **Printing and paper:** woodblock printing (blocks carved with copper and silver tools); paper
  from wood pulp, made easy by lye.

## 3b. The First Sailors

### A world-spanning empire
The First Sailors (the Builders) ruled a powerful **world-spanning empire**. Their elite, the
**Navigator Lords**, had achieved **true immortality** through magic. Believing **the soul lives in
the eyes**, each Navigator Lord placed **enchanted coral eyes in their moai** as a phylactery. They
stayed in the prime of physical health and could have children normally.

### The math, and the Crew
They **did the math**: a race of true immortals having children at normal rates would strip the
earth. So they bred deliberately slowly, as far as their technology allowed, and carried out **mass
cullings of children**. They euphemistically called the children **Crew**, used them as slaves, and
**sacrificed them around age 9–10 (Yoon's age)**, when they believed innocence began to end. A
Crewmember was only allowed to join the Navigators **when a Navigator Lord died**.

The reason is understandable, and still monstrous.

### Queen Tou
The last non-puppet monarch died with a single heir: a nine-year-old girl, whom the court sacrificed
and placed into an articulated wooden doll, her soul held in **coral eyes in a moai in her throne
room**. Queen Tou was kept as a **sacred innocent puppet ruler** and lived a pampered existence.

- The Navigator Lords told her the doll body was **life-preserving**, because she was deathly ill.
- They fed her **false news** of the outside world and **fake disasters** for her to sign decrees
  to "solve", and a rotating pool of **"noble children" to foster** who would "go home" after a
  year or two.
- Over hundreds of years a bored court invented absurdly varied and detailed petitions and crises,
  and eventually made the "results" of her past decrees build on each other realistically. She was
  effectively **role-playing a ruler for centuries**, which ironically makes her **a good ruler for
  the Children of the Caves**.

### The fall
The empire was overthrown by **a mass slave revolt of children** and their various creations, who
**shattered the statues' eyes** (hopefully all of them). These children, twisted by magic and
experiments, became **the Children of the Caves**. Tou's palace was buried by an eruption avalanche,
and she slept, unaware, for thousands of years, still "ruling".

When bored Children of the Caves dig her up, they think she's neat and start "serving" her. **With
the player's help she eventually learns what happened**, and is horrified: she knew nothing of what
her civilization really did.

### Glyph magic
**Spell names are glyph names**, and spells are cast by **reading the glyph aloud**. The
All-Seafarers read them **from scrolls**; the Islanders read them **from their own skin** (their
tattoos). **Tou** (a First Sailor), and **Falmi** and **Lasa** (innately magical), use glyphs
**without any medium**.

**Ghost Yoon** uses only the unique ghost abilities; Yoon learns regular magic and weapon skills
after gaining a body.

### Demons
**Demons are servants and creations of the Destroyer**, as the People of the Forest are of the
Ladies, but greater, because the Destroyer is a far more powerful god of an **earlier generation**.

## 3c. The World and the All-Seafarers

### The world is a bowl
The known world is **a large sea ringed by a single continent** that rises quickly into very high,
jagged mountains. Most people live on islands in the sea. **The whole world is a truly immense
impact crater**: the Red Bowl myth is literally true.

### The All-Seafarers
- **Homeland:** the jagged, marginal coasts on the crater rim, which is where they get **tin** (and
  so bronze, which the Islanders lack).
- **Ideology:** the King of All the Sea believes the gods sent him a vision that he is destined to
  conquer "All the Sea", which is the entire world as far as anyone knows.
- **The royal family:** the King has literally dozens of sons and daughters by a comically large
  number of wives, and uses them as his administrators and governors.
- **Relations with the island:** a **tributary relationship**, paid for **about 30 years** on Juda's
  advice. **At the start of the game relations are peaceful**, and neither side pushes the point: the
  Seafarers consider the island a **vassal polity**; the Islanders see the Seafarers as **"rich,
  friendly strangers we trade with"**.
- **The Seafarer presence before the invasion** is small:
  - A **little garrison in Zonfahare**.
  - A few **ships docked at Mansehare**, with **warehouses** and dock infrastructure.
  - **Iras**, the tributary official, **married to an Islander woman**.
  - **Warhus**, the dock-master at Mansehare.

### Theenmy's plan
Theenmy fully believes in his father's destiny and loves him, but also happens to be a decent man.
His plan for tighter control over the island: make **Juda a puppet ruler** and **marry Juda to one of
his sisters**, who would be the real ruler. Relatively peaceful, few casualties, a smooth
integration; the "primitive" islanders would be "civilized" while keeping a voice.

**The demon ritual was Juda's own idea**, to get rid of those who would oppose the plan (like
Pada). Theenmy knew nothing of it and would have balked immediately.

**The sister:** whatever happens with Theenmy, the sister who was to marry Juda grows frustrated
with the whole situation and **seizes control of the invasion force in a coup**, choosing to
subjugate the island through straightforward brutality (the Princess of Act 5).

### Parl
A crewman on the All-Seafarer **tribute collector's** ship, from **a big city in the All-Seafarer
homeland**, and also a **literate spy**: his job was to write down and record things about the Islanders
before the invasion. He stole some of the **tribute pearls**, meaning to hide them on the ship and fence
them back in the capital. He was found out and deserted into the highlands. **Chief Tama** and the
Seafarer tributary official **Iras** tasked **Dama** and a few of her **spearwives** (Mansehare's
informal militia) with finding out how he stole the pearls and tracking him down.

## 3d. Yoon, Their Family, and the Murder

### Yoon's family
- **Mada:** Yoon's mother, the **village herbalist** (considered **the best on the island**), lame in one leg. Keeps some of her own blood for
  ritual potions, which is where the body's ritual gets hers.
- **Pada:** Yoon's father, considered **the best hunter on the island**.
- **The twins:** Yoon's younger twin siblings, a boy and a girl. As twins they can see ghosts: they
  see Yoon, they're the ones who tell Pada that Yoon is a ghost, and they give their blood for the body.
- **Ladder:** the family goat, named because he climbs ladders. Somehow.
- **Patience:** the family dog.

**The family home** stands on a **rocky eyot in the river just north of Zonfahare proper**, on the edge of
town where four lands meet: the town, the lowland forest, the grassland and the marsh. A hunter and an
herbalist live where they can reach every kind of game and plant, a little apart from the town itself.

**Yoon's expected path (flavour only):** a boy would be expected to follow Pada as a hunter, a girl
Mada as an herbalist, and a third-gender or nonbinary child would be expected to become a priest. Yoon's
gender and appearance are the player's choice; this only colours dialogue.

### Yoon and Sib
Best friends since basically birth. They liked to play in **the lava tubes near the village**. Sib has
a **massive puppy-love crush** on Yoon, whatever gender the player chooses. Whether Yoon returns it is
up to the player, and they're kids: it never goes beyond **holding hands**, and at most a **childhood
betrothal promise** in certain endings.

- Sib **never suspected Juda** before Act 3.
- After Act 3, Sib becomes the village priest **de facto**: Juda is gone and the island is being invaded.

### The murder (what really happened)
1. **Juda kidnapped Yoon** while they were playing alone in the lava tubes.
2. He **clubbed Yoon over the head with his ceremonial staff, topped with a copper ball**, crushing
   Yoon's skull.
3. He took the body to **an isolated tomb only he knew about**, **defleshed** Yoon, and **burned the
   flesh in the tomb over a grave** as an offering to summon a demon.
4. He felt regret at killing a child he had known for about ten years, which **"ruined" the ritual**.
5. He left with his **broken staff**: he **dumped the copper ball in the marsh** and **buried the
   staff in the woods**. (Patience later digs up the staff, and the player unknowingly uses it as a
   weapon.)

On the mountain, Juda **burns Pada to death**.

### How the mystery resolves
**The player can't "solve" the murder directly.** Instead, **the more evidence the player collects,
the sooner Dama arrives** in the fight against Juda after he burns Pada to death on the mountain,
and **the better armed and prepared the villagers with her are**, so **Juda kills fewer NPCs** before
"dying".

- **Kavakava figures:** rib-thin carved ancestor figures guarding First Sailor tombs. Weak to fire and

- **Possessed animals:** demons can ride any carcass, not only dogs: a dead aurochs as a battering

**Pranksters.** The Children of the Caves and the forest child-fae are enemies in **prank fights**:
- They **never knock anyone out**, and when beaten they **run off** instead of dying.
- Children of the Caves **steal shiny things** (copper, silver and bronze gear, cowries) and **pull clothes

- Stolen things go to **hidden hoards** in the lava tubes, found later. That's a reason to explore, and

mechanic.)

**Elements follow the domains** in general: forest beings (the Woods, Earth) resist earth and take
extra fire; the sea and the Red Bowl lean Water; the Lady of the Wind's marsh and coasts lean Air; the
Sun and the Destroyer lean Fire.

## 4. Inferences (assistant's reading; to confirm or correct)

### Real-world roots
- **Rapa Nui** shapes the First Sailors: the moai with coral eyes; the **Stonemen as the unfinished
  moai of the Rano Raraku quarry** (carved from tuff, with red scoria topknots); an undeciphered
  script (First Sailor inscriptions); **Sooty Bird and the Birdman cult** of the sooty tern; a
  civilization that collapsed.
- **The Guanche of the Canary Islands** shape the Islanders and the cosmology: a volcanic island
  dominated by its volcano, a people who mummified their dead, conquered by a metal-armed sea power.
  (Orchil red dye, a historic Canary Islands export, fits too.) **The Destroyer stealing a star
  into the volcano closely mirrors Guayota**, the demon in Teide who
  imprisoned the sun god Magec, and **Guayota's servants, the Tibicenas, were demonic black dogs**.
- **Basque and wider Iberian folklore** seem to shape the local spirits: the Shepherd of the Deer
  echoes Basajaun, the Stream Maidens the lamiak, the sleep demon Inguma, and the Lady of the Woods,
  living in two places at once, echoes Mari moving between her mountain homes.
- **The Dream Hunters** match Corsica's mazzeri.
- **Spell language:** spell roots are Rapa Nui and modifiers Guanche. Perhaps the **roots are the old
  First Sailor sacred tongue** and the **modifiers are living Islander speech**: magic as ancient
  words bent by present-day grammar.

### History in layers
1. **Cosmic age:** the Star Mother, the Star Child's death and scattering, White Bird's crime and
   penance, the Destroyer's theft of the star (the volcano), and the Sun and Moon.
2. **The First Sailors (the Builders):** the Star Child's shooting-star body made flesh; a
   world-spanning empire ruled by immortal **Navigator Lords** whose souls live in coral moai eyes.
   Culled their own children (the Crew) to keep an immortal population in check, with a sacrificed
   child queen as puppet. **Overthrown by a children's revolt** that shattered the moai eyes; the
   palace was buried by an eruption. Remnants: Stonemen, ancient ghosts, Hags, the wells' snake women, owls on
   their towers, the Children of the Caves, Tou, and Juda.
3. **The Islanders (now):** arrived centuries after the First Sailors vanished. Copper Age
   metallurgy but electrochemical technology; living by negotiated agreements with the Ladies; a
   third gender priesthood grounded in the genderless Red Bowl and Star Child; the dead as neighbors.
4. **The All-Seafarers (arriving):** a Bronze Age sea empire whose theology claims the Red Bowl as
   their king's father, and who misread island reality through their own myths (releasing the Bull
   of Water).

### Threads that tie the story together
- **Homunculi:** the Children of the Caves are discarded First Sailor homunculi, and Yoon's new body
  is a homunculus built with the same kind of craft. Lasa, a homunculus descendant who doesn't know
  it, helps build another. The Tou questline likely brings this into the open.
- **Child sacrifice and the children who survive it:** Tou (sacrificed child queen), the Children of
  the Caves (discarded children), Yoon (murdered child), the Star Child (the first murdered child),
  and Falmi (a child grown, not born).
- **Juda the binder:** he chained the Lady of the Sun and the Bull of Heaven to take the high
  priesthood and silence his accuser; the snake women in First Sailor wells foreshadow this binding
  magic. Freeing the bound is a recurring act of justice.
- **Mirrors:** White Bird ↔ Juda (killer, remorse, consequences); Star Child ↔ Yoon (generative
  death).
- **Four domains, four elements (a guess):** Fire ↔ Sun and Destroyer; Water ↔ Red Bowl and sea;
  Wind ↔ Lady of the Wind; Earth ↔ Lady of the Woods. If intended, element choices for bosses and
  spirits could follow their domains.

### Talk system ideas the lore suggests
- **Boar-men want honesty:** a plain-speaking approach should work and a flattering or deceptive one
  should backfire. That may mean adding **Flatter / Deceive** approaches (perhaps taught by the
  Master of Deception) that work on some beings and backfire on others.
- **Hunched:** a new outcome, **Release** (laying the corpse to rest), as the mercy alternative to
  destroying them.
- **Ghosts:** Yoon gets a ghost-kinship bonus (a specialty for Yoon after all, or an enemy
  `<Talk Instant>`-style affinity).

### Where magic and skills come from
- **The Master of Deception** teaches priests in exchange for **dreams**: a source for Sib's

- **The Shepherd of the Deer** teaches **stealth magic** in exchange for **songs**: stealth for

### From the Islanders (extrapolations to confirm)
- **Electricity is Burn damage.** Islander "weapons" are tools: shock prods, fire-starting wire,
  lye and chlorine (acid/poison Burn), **electric fences** as traps against invader patrols. The
  Voltaic Pile accessory is Islander tech; Conducting Edge marries it to (imported?) metal swords.
- **Medicine is advanced for the era:** disinfectant, electric **cautery** (an item that stops Bleed
  at a small HP cost), surgery. Healing items can be Islander medicine; **mild current** could even
  be a rare revival item.
- **Electroplating** could be an equipment upgrade: a battery tender silver-plates a copper weapon,
  making it better against spirits and the dead (if silver matters to them).
- **The telegraph** is a strategic network in the invasion (defend or cut lines, warn hamlets) and a
  source of **mystery clues** (message logs).
- **Printing and customary bureaucracy** mean **records exist**: births, deaths, apprenticeships.
  **Juda's false orphan backstory** is a natural clue: his records are missing, forged, or
  impossibly old. Printed notices, letters and **Juda's notes** (which taught Sib the forbidden
  homunculus ritual) can all be found documents.
- **Seismographs** let the village watch the volcano: foreshadowing for the Destroyer.
- **Lighting** for dark lava tubes (the areas with random encounters): lamps and battery-powered
  light as exploration tools.
- **Ghost sight:** since only priests see ghosts unaided, non-priests may need **help** (an item,
  ritual or state) to see or target ghost enemies. Sib and Juda see them by default.
- **Defleshing as a clue:** cut marks on the bones point to someone practiced at preparing bodies,
  and priests are the island's mummifiers.
- **Tattoos as a progression slot:** earned glyphs with true-name effects (passive bonuses), with
  forbidden Destroyer glyphs as powerful, corrupting options. Learning glyphs ties into Yoon's
  comprehension (Insight) and First Sailor inscriptions.
- **Copper-silver weapons:** more durable wear tiers, plus a small magical part in every hit (a
  weapon-level damage profile addition).
- **Aurochs** as rare, dangerous wild cattle to hunt.
- **Cowries** as currency; pearls and ingots as sellable crafting materials.
- **Economy:** dye, pearls, silver and copper are what an empire would want. The **mines** and the
  dye trade are a likely reason for the invasion.
- **Food items** from the crops and herds: bread, lentil stew, wine, cheese.

### From the First Sailors (extrapolations to confirm)
- **Juda's survival is explained.** He burns to ash in Act 3 and returns in Act 5: as a Navigator
  Lord, his soul lives in **coral eyes in a moai that survived the revolt**. Killing him for good in
  Act 6 means **finding and destroying his moai's eyes**: a natural Act 6 quest, and a boss with a
  "Remote Body" like Tou's until the eyes are broken.
- **The Navigator-Lord remnants** are likely **souls in moai whose eyes were not all shattered**: the
  "coral eyes watching" behind the Stonemen. Each surviving moai is a potential hidden boss or
  secret, and a threat Juda could wake.
- **Yoon's age is the Crew sacrifice age.** Juda murdered a child at exactly the age his
  civilization culled them: he is still practicing the old rite.
- **Tou's decrees as a mechanic:** she spent centuries issuing decrees, so a unique battle command
  (issuing a "decree" as a party buff or an order to First Sailor beings) and an overworld one
  (directing the Children of the Caves to dig, scout or fetch) would fit her.
- **Spells as glyphs** (decided; see concept.md "Spell acquisition"): Islanders through **tattoo**
  slots, All-Seafarers through a **codex**, Tou, Falmi and Lasa innately.
  - **Yoon's "Infernal" skills** could be **Destroyer glyphs**: the demon in Yoon showing through.
  - **Yoon's homunculus body starts without the old Yoon's tattoos**, a quiet loss.
- **Demons and Yoon:** if Yoon is a demon, Yoon is a creation of the Destroyer, which is why the
  Hags (demon-possessed) recognize kin and dote on Yoon.

### From the world and the All-Seafarers (extrapolations to confirm)
- **The "invasion" is an escalation,** from a twenty-year tributary relationship to direct rule.
  All-Seafarer traders and tribute collectors could already be a familiar sight in Act 1–2.
- **Bronze is an imperial monopoly** because tin comes from the rim; that is why the Islanders are a
  copper culture and why bronze gear is unbreakable, prestigious and foreign.
- **Juda's collaboration leaves evidence:** letters, codices, tribute records. **Parl can read them**,

- **The Princess was to marry Juda.** When Juda kills her in Act 5 he kills his own intended bride
  and patron, which makes the reveal colder.
- **A crater-rim world** gives the map a shape: the island near the bowl's center? The rim as the
  far, mountainous homeland of the empire.

### From the pantheon (extrapolations to confirm)
- **The royal family has divine blood.** Theenmy and the Princess are grandchildren of a Lady, and
  so great-grandchildren of Star Mother. Theenmy's paladin magic is his heritage. The empire calls
  the Ladies' family thieves and criminals while being ruled by their descendants.
- **The King's founding adventure is a dark mirror of the player's party.** The heroes of an
  earlier JRPG became the empire, and their "righting wrongs" meant chaining and conscripting gods.
  Juda was one of those heroes, and chaining the Lady of the Sun was the same program applied to
  the island.
- **Juda's history with the empire is 65 years old**: the beloved "old wizard" of the founding
  legend. Theenmy would have grown up on stories of him.
- **Juda's pattern is fake deaths and new identities.** That suggests clues: the teenage mystic's
  arrival 50 years ago, the convenient death of the previous priest who chose him, and records that
  don't add up.
- **The Ladies fighting for the Princess** are bound gods, natural Act 5 bosses that can be **freed
  rather than killed** (through Talk), in line with the game's theme of freeing the bound. If they are
  held with First Sailor chain magic, Juda likely gave the empire that knowledge.
- **Idols as world infrastructure:** Red Bowl (save), Star Mother (heal and rest), Bird (fast travel
  along the telegraph network), Star Child (graveyards). Star Child idols could matter to ghosts and
  to Yoon.
- **The island is the center of the world (confirmed):** this is why gods, the empire and the
  Navigator Lords all converge here.
