# Smoking Isle — Game Concept

A narrative JRPG/CRPG hybrid built in RPG Maker MV. It is a murder mystery set on a
Copper Age volcanic island. You play the ghost of a dead child investigating your own
murder, while your best friend, the village priest's apprentice, helps you do
forbidden necromancy to build you a new body.

See also **[lore.md](lore.md)** for the gods, spirits and history of the island.

---

## Story Outline

### Prologue / Act 1 — The Ghost
- **Opening:** a cold open narrated by **Thenmy** (the player isn't told who), over black or slow
  pictures: *"My father once said that at the center of the world, there is an island that birthed The
  Sun..."* through *"I am told there will be very little blood."* Fade out; drip, drip, drip. Then the
  player is simply controlling a ghost in a cave, with no explanatory text. (Maps 5 "Prologue" and 6
  "Lava Tube: Waking".)
- You "wake up" as a spirit over a skeletal corpse, with no memories and no language.
- You move around as a spirit, learning language with Lasa's help and piecing together that
  you "are" the spirit of a missing (actually dead) child the village is mourning.
- No combat in this act.
- Ends when Yoon and Lasa make contact with Sib.

### Act 2 — The Body
- Yoon teams up with Sib, "their" best friend from when they were alive and the ward and
  apprentice of the village priest Juda, to perform a forbidden ritual that builds Yoon a new
  body from materials found all over the island.
- Patience joins alongside Sib.
- **Where the act goes:** lava tube → town → fields → marsh → lowland forest → highlands up to the snow line.
- **The body's ingredients, in the order they're gathered:**
  1. **A bone from Yoon's own skeleton**: the first quest, right after the Ghost and Lasa run into Sib and
     Sib realises who the Ghost is. Yoon, Sib and Lasa take it from the remains in the lava tube where the
     ghost woke, and that's when the player **learns for sure it was foul play**: the bones were prepared
     like a sacrifice, and the skull was smashed from behind (common event 12, "Scene: The Bone").
  2. Around the **town**, the **fields** near it and the **marsh**, in any order:
     - **A bit of pure silver**, worked into a bracelet.
     - **A pearl from the sea**, set into the bracelet → anchors the soul. **Tracking down Parl is the first
       big quest and boss.** The party hears that one of the Seafarers has stolen some of the tribute pearls
       (Dama and her spearwives are investigating for Chief Tama and the Seafarer official Iras). How they
       deal with him decides whether he's dead, an enemy, or an ally in Act 4, and Dama's attitude later on.
     - **Blood from Yoon's kin** → the blood. The **twins** (who can see Yoon) give theirs; **Mada** keeps
       some of her own for ritual potions; **Pada** can add his once he joins.
  3. **Pada joins.** The twins have told him Yoon is a ghost; he's been worried about the children since
     Yoon's "disappearance", and suspicious of why Sib, normally *not* an outdoorsy child, keeps traipsing
     around. He comes and finds Lasa, Sib and the Ghost; they tell him everything (Yoon is dead, a ghost, and
     they're attempting a forbidden ritual), and he agrees to help find the rest of the ingredients **and to
     deal with the Lady of the Woods** for permission to hunt the Great Boar and harvest a dragon tree.
  4. In the **lowland forest**, with the Lady's leave:
     - **Flesh of the Great Boar** → the flesh (the act's climactic boss).
     - **A framework of dragon-tree wood** → the skeleton.
     - **A living bellflower**, kept magically alive → the heart.
  5. Up through the **highlands to the snow line**: **snow**, trapped in an urn and kept magically frozen
     until the right moment → the brain.

### Act 3 — The Mountain
- Now in a physical body, Yoon sets out with Juda to gather materials for a ritual to restore
  their memories, then climbs the volcano to the ritual site.
- The ritual is a genuine attempt, but fails for ambiguous reasons.
- Dama storms up with Pada and a mob of villagers. They have discovered that Juda murdered Yoon.
- Juda kills Pada as a sacrifice, trying to banish Yoon. He believes Yoon is the demon he tried
  to summon through the murder, not the child's ghost. He never says so, leaving the player to
  believe he was only covering up the murder.
- **Juda is optionally beatable.** Bring him to 0 Health and he doesn't die: his skin splits and he rises
  in a **partial Navigator Lord form** (coral eyes), which the party can't fairly beat at this point. It
  beats them down without knocking anyone out, and after three turns the fire roars up and the battle
  breaks off. Switch 9 ("Juda half-risen") records that the player saw it, for later dialogue: a
  foreshadowing of the Act 5 reveal for players who earned it.
- Juda burns to ash in a fire he sets in the alpine forest.
- Dama joins. Pada is permanently gone, and so are any of his unfinished sidequests.

### Act 4 — The Invasion
- The All-Seafarers invade.
- Yoon teams up with Dama, and possibly Parl, to rally the island's forces against them.
- The Familiar and Last Queen questlines unlock (and can be done any time after this point).
- At the end of the act, the party discovers that Thenmy, the invading Prince, isn't evil, and
  can choose to **surrender to him, fight him, or negotiate with him**.

### Act 5 — The Princess
- A second All-Seafarer force arrives under Thenmy's sister, who is far worse.
- Thenmy joins the party.
- Her army wreaks havoc: freeing the blue bull, trying to burn down the forests, and more.
- A boss fight built up as the final battle. She surrenders, and either lets Thenmy govern
  (possibly marrying Dama) or leaves with him to keep the capital under control when the King
  of All Seas dies.
- **Twist:** Juda reappears, horribly scarred but alive. He kills the Princess and absorbs her
  life force to restore his body. He reveals that he is a living Navigator Lord and a servant of
  the Destroyer, who waited for a war on the island so he could build his master a new army.
  (There is room for a confrontation between him and Tou here.)

### Act 6 — The Destroyer
- The game shifts into a more traditional JRPG structure.
- The party crosses the whole island to find a way into the Destroyer's lair.
- They fight the Destroyer's armies, then Juda, then the Destroyer as the true final boss.
- **Endgame Juda is a four-stage boss**, peeling off his four identities from newest to oldest:
  1. **The village priest**, with Islander spells (Sib's moment).
  2. **The wandering young mystic** who murdered the priest who took him in.
  3. **The old wizard of the King's party**: Thenmy learns, mid-fight, who his father's companion was.
  4. **The Navigator Lord**, his true form, soul in coral eyes: fully what the Act 3 form only hinted at
     (Tou's moment).

### Juda's Layers (for reference while writing)
1. The respected, wise village priest and Sib's master.
2. Murdered Yoon so the death of an innocent child who trusted him would summon a demon. The
   ritual failed because he felt regret at the last moment.
3. Genuinely unsure whether Yoon is a harmless demon from the ruined ritual, or Yoon's ghost
   with its memory lost because part of the soul was siphoned off.
4. Secretly collaborating with the All-Seafarer invaders...
5. ...while really planning to use the demon to kill the invaders once they landed...
6. ...while *really* being a millennia-old First Sailor/Builder Navigator Lord, using the war as
   a source of bodies for demon summoning and necromancy for his master, the Destroyer, the evil
   volcanic deity.

### Yoon — The Protagonist
The ghost of a murdered child who has lost all memories from before death. Or possibly a demon
summoned by Juda through the child's murder, who believes it is the child. Their quest to find
out who they were, learn language, recover their body and recover their memories spirals from
there. The player chooses Yoon's gender (Yoon's **looks are the same** whichever is chosen): boy, girl, or the island's priestly **third
gender**, which the game and the village translate with **they/them** (as for Sib; see lore.md, "Priests").

**What Yoon's gender changes** (`SI_Yoon.js`; mostly flavor, a few small effects):

| | Boy | Girl | Third gender |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pronouns | he / him / his | she / her / her | they / them / their |
| How people address Yoon | son, lad | daughter, lass | child, little priest |
| Expected job as a kid | Hunter, like Pada | Herbalist, like Mada | Priest |
| Closer to | Pada | Mada | Both |
| Morbid childhood hobby (why the eeriness isn't new) | Butchering the game from Pada's hunts | Vet work on the family's animals with Mada | Augury: reading omens and entrails with the priests |
| Small effect | **Stalk +20**; animals start a little friendlier | **Remedies Yoon uses heal 20% more** | **Rite +20**; spirits start a little friendlier |
| In Yoon's room (with a body) | The goat-hair sling Pada made, a tusk from the first hunt | A jar of leeches Mada kept alive, herbs Yoon dried | Sandalwood beads from the priests, the gourd rattle Sib traded for |

Dialogue writes these with text codes (`\YP[they]`, `\YV[are|is]`, `\YG[son|daughter|child]`; see the
editor guide). **Naming:** the player names Yoon at the start and every other party member the first time
they join (`SI_Naming.js`).

**Ghost or demon is genuinely ambiguous, and the game never answers it.** The evidence supports both
readings. In dialogue the player can have Yoon (and the people around them) lean either way, or stay
agnostic; the game respects whichever stance the player takes and never confirms it.

**Personality: cute, in a feral, uncanny way.** Yoon reads as a sweet, curious child, until they don't: the
"may actually be a demon puppeting a child homunculus" reading is always one line away. **Some dialogue
options are scary or way out there**, and the player can pick them. Examples of the register:
- Ghost Yoon, learning words: *"Sib. Sib. Sib. Is that what you taste like?"*
- To a frightened villager: *"You can't see me. That's all right. I can see you very well."*
- Looking at their new body for the first time: *"It fits. I wonder whose it was."*
- To the Great Boar: *"Hold still. I want to be you for a little while."*
- To Juda, any time before the truth comes out: *"You smell like the dark where I woke up."*
- Comforting Sib: *"If anyone hurts you I'll put them under the mountain. ...Is that not what friends say?"*
These sit beside ordinary, sweet and funny options, never instead of them. Other characters react: Sib laughs
nervously, Lasa takes it as ghost talk, Pada goes quiet, the twins giggle, Juda watches very carefully.
They feed the ghost-or-demon ambiguity without ever settling it.

**And it's not that big a leap from before.** Living Yoon was already a bit off-putting: a child happily
elbow-deep in something. As a boy, butchering the game from Pada's hunts; as a girl, doing vet work on the
family's animals with Mada; as third gender, augury with the priests (reading omens and entrails). So when
Yoon says something ghoulish, the people who knew them can't tell whether it's the demon or just *Yoon*.
That's both the joke and the horror.

**It's also why Yoon wears a pelt** (the orange fur at the shoulder in their art): the same pelt whatever the
gender, worn for that old hobby. As a boy, a hide he skinned and cured himself from one of Pada's kills; as a
girl, the pelt of an animal she nursed and couldn't save, and wouldn't let go of; as third gender, an
augur's mantle from the priests, for reading omens. Their new body is dressed in it when they get it back.

**The new body** is **lankier** than Yoon was, with **red eyes** (the ritual scrolls said it could do that).
The scene where Yoon wakes in it is common event 11, "Scene: Yoon's New Body": Sib, Lasa and Pada react,
and Yoon answers *"Being in a body itches."*, *"I smell like blood and pork."* or *"This is nice. I feel
warm. Being a ghost was cold."*

- **Weapons (Human form):** knives, staffs/spears, slings. Swords if Thenmy's sidequest is
  completed. Bows if all of Pada's sidequests are completed before his death.
- **Ghost form:** very low HP. Dodges nearly all physical attacks and takes almost no damage from
  those that connect. Extremely vulnerable to magic. Cannot deal damage directly. Has all-enemy
  debuffs, all-party buffs and an enormous MP pool. Cannot equip weapons.
- **Human form:** slightly tankier than Sib physically. Single-target buffs and debuffs replace
  the all-target ghost versions. MP is much lower than in Ghost form but still fairly high. Learns
  weaker versions of other party members' abilities through relationship sidequests. The
  demon/homunculus body is stronger than a normal child's, so Yoon can use two-handed weapons and
  heavy helms and greaves (but not heavy body armor or large shields).
- **Core role:** support and generalist. Never the best at anything, but potentially competent
  at everything depending on sidequest completion. The player's investment in relationships
  directly determines combat ability.
- Cannot die or leave.

### Sib — The Young Priest
Yoon's best friend since basically birth, who wants to help Yoon get a body back. Intersex, and
holds a third-gender social role that is very common for priests for that reason. **They/them.** Has a massive
puppy-love crush on Yoon (returned or not, as the player chooses; kids, so never beyond holding hands and,
in certain endings, a childhood betrothal promise). Juda's apprentice; never suspects him. After Act 3,
the village priest de facto, since Juda is gone and the island is being invaded.

**Family:** born into the **Lightning Clan**. Because they're intersex, they were offered to the
priesthood, and at **8** went to live with Juda. They still talk to and love their parents: **Uira**
(their mother), the **telegraph operator** in Zonfahare, and **Poko** (their father), its **battery
tender**. As a child of the clan, Sib keeps the right to learn the Lightning Clan's techniques, which is
why a priest jars lightning too.

**Body (writer's background):** Sib's intersex condition is **45,X/46,XY mosaicism** (mixed gonadal
dysgenesis): some cells XY, some a single X. That's why the village knew at birth (ambiguous genitals) and
offered them to the priesthood. It explains their look and their frailty:
- **Androgynous, leaning feminine:** weak, late or partial puberty keeps a soft, in-between look.
- **Short and slight.**
- **A bit frail:** Turner-like heart differences (a bicuspid aortic valve or a narrowed aorta) mean low
  stamina and getting winded easily: among the lowest Health in the party, and tiring fast. Kidney differences
  and catching minor illnesses fit too. Mind and spirit are unaffected.
- **Ears:** a childhood of ear infections left them a little deaf on one side, so Sib tilts their head
  to listen. Fitting for a priest who "hears" spirits.
- **Words over spaces:** strong with words (reads every inscription, memorises rites, argues fluently),
  weaker with spatial puzzles: the one who gets lost in the lava tubes.
- **A quiet fear that they "look weird":** short, soft, in-between, with small odd details they notice in
  themselves. It feeds their certainty that Yoon couldn't want feelings from "someone like them".
In the game nobody names it: the islanders see a child the gods marked as a priest.

**The crush:** Sib has a **big** crush on Yoon, whatever gender the player chooses.
- The village sees nothing wrong with it.
- **Sib keeps it secret from Yoon**, sure that Yoon wouldn't want feelings from someone like them.
- **The player can't shut it down cruelly.** The worst Yoon can say is that they only see Sib as a best
  friend. Returned or not, it stays kids' puppy love: never beyond holding hands, and at most a childhood
  betrothal promise in certain endings.

- **Weapons:** knives, staffs and slings, plus the priests' Jasper Knife and Jasper Spear. A narrow
  selection: a child trained for spiritual work, not combat.
- **Quirks:** among the lowest HP in the party, and in practice the most fragile: Lasa and Tou have similar
  Health but dodge by flying or come back after two turns; Sib has neither, and tires fastest. Largest and most sustainable MP pool. Widest
  spell list, which keeps growing as Sib learns magic without a teacher. Advantages in spirit and
  monster pre-combat encounters through ritual knowledge and relationships with forest spirits.
  Gets physically tired quickly.
- **Core role:** main healer and magic damage dealer. Fragile, essential and irreplaceable. When
  Sib goes down, the party is in immediate crisis.
- Cannot die or leave.

### Lasa — The Child of the Caves
A girl of the Children of the Caves, a fae-like race that only appears normally to children.
She finds Yoon as a ghost and immediately decides to help, **out of the goodness of her heart**: she
thinks Yoon is just a lost ghost who needs showing the way. No bargain, no hidden motive. It's what the
Children of the Caves do for those in desperate need when all other help has failed.

**A guardian.** Islanders believe every child is given a guardian at birth, a Child of the Caves or a Child
of the Woods, and to the Children of the Caves that's real: each of them watches over an Islander child.
Lasa is one of those guardians, and **she was Yoon's**. Yoon was killed while playing in the lava tubes, her
home, and she wasn't there to stop it. When she finds the ghost she **doesn't recognise them** and helps
purely because they seem lost. Learning later that the lost ghost she adopted was her own child is one of
her biggest moments: guilt she has to live with, and a reason she never leaves Yoon's side.
- **Why she wasn't there:** both. She was off after something shiny when Yoon was taken, and by the time
  she noticed, Yoon had vanished behind Juda's ward: his tomb is sealed with First Sailor glyphs that blind
  the Children of the Caves (First Sailor creations themselves). She searched and couldn't see through it.
- **When she realises:** when Yoon gets a body (end of Act 2). The new body takes after the family's
  blood, and when she sees the face she watched over for ten years, it clicks.
  (In common event 11: *"Y-you! You're. You're my kid. Oh no. Oh no oh no."*)
- **What guardians know:** a face, not a name. Lasa never knew Yoon's name. Before the murder she **felt
  something weird around Yoon** (Juda, though she couldn't tell); then a **"slippery magic wall"** (his
  ward) hid Yoon from her; then **the wall went away** and she found a ghost she didn't connect with her
  child.

- **Weapons:** knives only (small hands).
- **Quirks:** flies, so she dodges most melee attacks. Decent ATK, lowish HP, high MP and a small
  set of useful spells.
- **Core role:** early damage crutch, debuffer and minor magic damage dealer. Helps Sib and Yoon
  collect parts for Yoon's new body.
- With Yoon from the start, teaching them language. Fights once Sib joins.
- **A permanent party member** once Sib joins (no longer leaves when Juda arrives). The only one who can
  gather cave plants.

### Pada — The Hunter / The Father
Yoon's father, a mighty hunter respected by the Islanders.

- **Weapons:** knives, spears, axes, clubs/maces, paddle-swords, bows. The widest selection in the early
  game.
- **Quirks:** by far the highest HP and ATK in the early party. Very limited magic. Advantages in
  animal pre-combat encounters through tracking and wilderness skill. **Adult protection:** takes
  a blow for a child party member whose HP drops below a threshold.
- **Core role:** tank, physical damage dealer and party protector. His presence makes everything
  feel safe, and his absence makes everything feel dangerous.
- Joins in Act 2 after the Parl quest, once the twins have told him Yoon is a ghost: worried about the children and
  suspicious of Sib's sudden trips, he hears from Yoon's twins (who can see ghosts) that Yoon is a
  ghost, gets the rest from Sib and Lasa, and agrees to help. Dies on
  the mountain. Any of his sidequests left unfinished are permanently missed.

### Patience — The Dog
Pada and Yoon's dog, a pointer-type breed.

- **Weapons:** teeth.
- **Quirks:** a **pseudo party member**, like Boney in *Mother 3* or King in *EarthBound*. She
  travels and fights alongside the party in addition to the four main slots, but isn't a full
  member: small passive benefits, occasional minor attacks or situational help.
  - **Acts on her own** (AI-controlled). Left alone she **Bites**, **Growls** (ATK down) and
    **Points** (the party's next damaging hit on that enemy is a critical).
  - **She does these first when they're needed:** **Shake Off** when she's Wet (dries herself and
    soaks an enemy), and **Nuzzle** when an ally (a child first) is in Despair or Panicked.
  - **Orders** are Talk skills only **Sib, Pada and Human Yoon** have (the people she actually listens
    to). Each costs half a turn and no Fatigue, and she carries it out on her next turn:
    - **"Heel, Girl!"**: she only guards until someone calls it again. Keeps her from biting (and
      angering) an enemy the party is talking down. Other orders still get through.
    - **"Sic 'Em!"** (pick an enemy): **Ankle Bite**, light damage that pushes its next turn back.
    - **"Catch 'Em!"** (pick an enemy): **Hold**, so it can't dodge, flee or run off with stolen
      things for 2 turns.
    - **"Fetch!"**: **Fetch** brings back a thrown javelin or harpoon (one more throw). With nothing
      thrown, she does as she likes.
    - **"Speak!"**: **Alarm Bark** if anyone is asleep (wakes the party), else **Howl** if there are
      spirits (they may panic), else **Growl**.
    Ankle Bite, Hold, Fetch, Howl and Alarm Bark are only ever used when ordered.
  - **Gear:** four slots of her own.
    - **Collar:** Woven, Hide, Shark-Tooth, Coral-Eyed, Glow-Bead (cave hit), Obsidian-Spiked (biters take
      30% back; resists Dragged Below; for the Black Hounds), Salt-Pouch (30% less damage from spirits),
      Bronze War-Dog.
    - **Pack:** Javelin Pack (the whole party gets +1 javelin throw), Medicine Pack (once a battle, she
      drags the first fallen ally back up at 1 HP), **Lamp Pack** (the party has a light in the dark),
      **Forager's Pack** (gathering's rare extras twice as likely), **Stone Pack** (+2 sling stones for everyone).
    - **Booties:** Hide (Burdened, Mired), Seal-Skin (Wet, Sodden), Wool (Chilled), Pitch-Hide
      (Smoldering, Enflamed), **Sharkskin** (immune to Bleed), **Deerskin** (Speed +3), **Felt** (25% less Fire).
    - **Bell:** Copper (no ambushes, but Stalk works 25% worse), Silver (spirits start friendlier),
      Shell (sea creatures start friendlier), **Clay** (she starts Calm), **Ringing-Stone** (phonolite: every enemy
      starts Disoriented). **Collar:** also the **Silver-Studded Collar** (30% less damage from demons).
  - **Has her own turns** in the Turn Order, based on her speed.
  - **Can be knocked out**, and is revived the same way as everyone else.
  - **Insight is capped at 1.** Her specialty (dogs and animals) works **passively**: with
    Patience present, animal conversations go better for whoever is speaking.
- Finds the buried murder weapon early in the game. The player uses it as a staff without knowing
  what it is.
- **Core role:** emotional anchor. The dog who waited, and the dog who doesn't care what you are.

### Juda — The Traitor
The village priest, deeply respected by the Islanders, very knowledgeable and wise. Sib's master.
(See Juda's Layers above.)

- **Weapons:** knives, staffs/spears, clubs/maces, slings.
- **Quirks:** powerful mage with a deep spell list and decent melee. Advantages in priestly
  pre-combat encounters through institutional authority and spiritual knowledge. Has the adult
  protection skill, which on a replay reads as protecting his ritual ingredients. Strong enough
  that the player truly values him, so the betrayal is a gameplay loss as well as a story one.
- **Core role:** temporary powerhouse who covers the children's weaknesses. His strength is the
  trap.
- A mandatory temporary guest during the memory ritual trek. Betrays the party, kills Pada and
  becomes an antagonist. The party loses his combat power exactly when they need it most.

### Dama — The Chieftess
Leads Mansehare's spearfishers and the **spearwives**, sworn fisherwomen who act as the island's investigators and law officers (lore.md, "Spearwives"). Her first case is Parl and the stolen pearls; once that's
closed she's put on Yoon's murder, and discovers that Juda did it. Yoon coming back changes nothing for
her: a child was lured into a tomb and butchered, and "this is unacceptable to The Mother and to decency". Becomes Chieftess by default over the course of the war.

- **Weapons:** knives, spears, paddle-swords, nets, javelins. Her **Fishing Gear** (ranged) holds one net cast
  and two javelin throws a battle; any harpoon adds one more throw.
- **Quirks:** tankier than the children, with good damage and strong debuffs through nets. Nets
  give her ranged battlefield control, slowing enemies rather than damaging them. Her **underwater
  breathing** spell opens submerged areas nobody else can reach. Has adult protection.
- Her relationship with the party depends on the pearl thief quest decisions made hours before
  she joins.
- **Core role:** the replacement anchor after Pada dies. Tank, battlefield controller, and the
  adult who chose to be here. Partly fills Pada's protective role, but with control rather than
  raw endurance.
- Cannot die or leave.

### Parl — The Pearl Thief
A crewman on the All-Seafarer tribute collector's ship and a literate spy, recording the Islanders
before the invasion. He stole some of the tribute pearls to fence back home, was found out, and
deserted into the salt marsh, where he hides. Chief Tama and the Seafarer tributary official Iras put Dama and her
spearwives (Mansehare's sworn investigators) on the case. A pearl is the fifth ingredient for Yoon's body, so hunting
him down is an important Act 2 quest.

- **Weapons:** knives, swords, spears, axes, slings or bows. A scrappy survivalist kit from months
  alone in the highlands.
- **Quirks:** survival specialist, thief and ranged fighter. Tankier than the children but the
  least tanky adult. High AGI. Decent at almost everything but best at nothing.
- **Intelligence:** speaks the enemy's language and has limited literacy in it. Can read captured
  orders, documents and supply manifests (otherwise only possible with the Prince). Knows
  ground-level All-Seafarer military operations, supply chains and naval procedures.
- **Core role:** flexible utility fighter and intelligence asset.
- **Recruitment, based on the pearl thief quest:**
  - **Option 1 (he turned himself in):** joins during the invasion with a good relationship with Dama.
  - **Option 2A (secret kept):** joins during the invasion, but Dama is hostile toward him.
  - **Option 2B (ratted out):** sides with the invaders and becomes an enemy. Never recruitable.
  - **Option 3 (pearls stolen, he dies):** never recruitable.

### Thenmy — The Prince
The Prince of the invaders, who always ends up siding with the party.

- **Weapons:** every melee type except staffs, including swords and javelins. His personal sword is a unique, irreplaceable masterwork bronze
  leaf sword. **Can swap melee weapons once per turn and still make a basic attack.**
- **Quirks:** highest HP and DEF in the game, very high ATK, lowest AGI (a bronze-armored
  glacier). Powerful paladin-style magic. **All his gear is bronze and therefore unbreakable**; his armour is **silver-plated**, as befits a prince, so it gleams white-silver rather than bronze.
  Has adult protection, the hint that he isn't so bad: the colonizer prince instinctively shields
  island children. His soldiers hesitate to attack children; his sister's soldiers do not.
- **Core role:** the best damage dealer and tank. Recruiting him transforms the party overnight,
  which is deliberately uncomfortable.
- **Recruitment:** at the climactic fight, by talking him down, defeating and sparing him, or
  surrendering to him. All three recruit him but change his attitude and relationships.

### Falmi — The Familiar / The Forest Prince
A child the Lady of the Woods (a minor deity and queen of the forest spirits) grew from a plant
over four hundred years to be her heir. Develops a crush on Tou.

- **Weapons:** knives, swords, paddle-swords, axes, clubs, spears, slings, javelins. The widest selection of any
  child. The only child who can use full-sized shields, including dragon-tree wood shields painted
  with geometric patterns.
- **Quirks:** a combat-focused child who tanks by avoiding hits. Average HP, high AGI,
  self-buffing magic and **HP regeneration every turn**, so their real staying power is far
  higher than their HP. Hard to knock out and keep down.
- **Literacy:** reads First Sailor inscriptions from growing up around ruins, with a different
  perspective from Sib (priestly) or the Prince (academic). Fully literate in Islander.
- **Core role:** the child tank, who makes an all-child party able to survive.
- **Recruitment:** through the Lady of the Woods questline. Falmi is "born" from the special herb
  she has cultivated for four hundred years.

### Tou — The Last Queen
The last queen of the First Sailors/Builders. A young girl the Navigator Lords used as a puppet
ruler, now trapped in a child-sized wooden doll with jointed limbs.

- **Weapons:** clubs, staffs, slings only. Bladed weapons are forbidden by a purity taboo the
  Navigator Lords imposed thousands of years ago, which she still keeps.
- **Quirks:** average HP and DEF. **Very weak to fire and cutting**, strongly resistant to blunt
  and piercing (it's a wooden body). **When knocked out, she comes back after two turns with
  reset TP**, because her soul is in the coral eyes of her moai in the throne room, not in the
  doll. This makes her the party member you can sacrifice tactically. Knows powerful First Sailor
  spells that others can only learn through hard inscription puzzles, but has a below-average MP
  pool: three or four big spells and she's empty.
- **First Sailor key:** deciphers First Sailor inscriptions perfectly, though their content may be
  hard for her emotionally. **Stonemen (First Sailor golems) surrender immediately** if she's in
  the party. Has advantages against First Sailor-era ghosts, the Hags, the Owls of Ill Omen and
  other ancient beings, and is useless against modern threats like the invasion.
- Cannot leave the island, because her soul is anchored to her moai.
- **Core role:** burst magic damage, the key to First Sailor content, and an emotional gut punch.
  With Yoon, Sib and Falmi she completes the all-child party: "the party of people the world
  wasn't designed for."
- **Recruitment:** optional. Found in her buried palace, where the player expects a terrifying
  boss fight and instead finds a lonely girl on an oversized throne telling fae creatures to
  behave. Can only be permanently killed if her moai's coral eyes are destroyed, which is a story
  threat rather than a gameplay mechanic.

### Aday — The Mechanist
The youngest child of **Beneharo** (the half-Seafarer electroplater of Celhare) and **Mahina** (Lightning
Clan), about 10: younger brother of Tahod and Tiare. Raised between the plating vats and the telegraph keys,
he takes everything apart, and he's the only kid on the island with bronze tools (scrounged from his
father's shop). In the invasion his family is distrusted by both sides, which is half of why he runs off
with the party: to prove whose side he's on.

- **Why he's required:** the party needs **a drill to dig deeper into the lava tubes**, and only Aday can
  design it.
- **The drill breaks the island's oldest *tapu*.** The deep tubes below the old tombs are **the
  Destroyer's**, and nobody digs there. **Atteneri** uses it against Sib ("a child priest letting a
  half-foreign child bore into the Destroyer's house"), some Islanders blame Aday's Seafarer blood, and Sib,
  as high priest, has to rule on it. Permission could be a scene: a rite at Jarcotapu, Itahisa's grudging
  blessing, an offering to the Star Mother.
- **Combat role: the item expert.** Remedies, meals and battle items work about 50% better in his hands;
  **Toss** throws one item over the whole party (the party's second source of healing); **Tinker** restores
  Voltaic charges mid-battle; gadgets (better Chlorine Jars, Pitch Firepots, Smoke Pots, Caltrops) with a
  chance not to use one up. Low Health and attack; nearly useless with an empty bag.
- **Outside battle:** repairs worn gear at camp and does simple metalwork without a smith: a walking
  crafting station. Turns scrap into something.
- **Taboos and customs:**
  - **The family-oath clash:** his mother's clan is sworn to **Sooty Bird** (only meat that flies), his
    father's side is Seafarer (**nothing in a shell**). So: bird meat, never eggs. *"Mum says birds. Grandpa
    says no shells. An egg is a bird AND a shell, so... no. I've thought about this a lot."*
  - **Never cut a line.** The clan's deepest rule: a telegraph wire is never cut, not even the enemy's. In
    Act 4 he refuses to cut the invaders' lines and finds another way (tap them, reroute them, feed them
    false messages).
  - **Clan secrets stay in the clan.** He won't tell outsiders how batteries are made (Kathrout asks
    constantly). Sib, clan by birth, is the only one he talks shop with.
  - **Nothing is scrap.** He throws nothing away.
  - From his father: he wears bronze, skips shell on days he's thinking of his grandfather, and bows to
    Thenmy without being told (then is embarrassed).

### Kathrout — The Scholar
A wealthy, independent Seafarer scholar from Keftiu, writing the first proper account of the Islanders.
Her name distorts **Katherine Routledge**, who led the 1914–15 expedition to Rapa Nui, recorded the birdman
cult and the islanders' oral history, credited her informants by name, and was caught between the islanders
and the company that ran the island during the 1914 uprising. Lives in Mansehare, next door to Iras. Her
sidequest chain fills the compendium (see lore.md, "People of the island").

- **Personality:** brisk and fearless; terrible at small talk, wonderful at listening; morally serious about
  understanding people; pedantic, and honest about her own mistakes. Her blind spot is the empire: she thinks
  a good book will make the Seafarers treat the island better, and doesn't yet see that a good book is also
  a very good map.
- **Her book becomes the invasion's map.** In Act 4 she finds it in an officer's tent, every telegraph
  station underlined, and has to choose: burn it, hide it, or give it to Dama. (She mirrors Parl, the other
  Seafarer who wrote everything down: "Welcome to the club.")
- **Combat role: pure mage.** She learned magic from books (anyone literate can use a Codex, and she's the
  most literate person on the island): she learns spells from First Sailor inscriptions and Glyph Tablet
  Shards, including glyphs others need quests for. Highest magic attack in the game, a big MP pool, frail for
  an adult. Tou keeps the First Sailor burst; Kathrout is the steady caster.
- **She can't see ghosts or spirits** (she's an adult): against spirits her spells go astray unless someone
  points the target out. (Lasa, oddly, she can see.)
- **Proposed arc, the illness** (after Routledge's last years, handled with care): guilt over the book and
  the war wear her down, and she starts hearing voices: her informants, the crying from the shearwater coast.
  The game never says whether it's spirits or illness (as with Yoon), and never makes it a monster or cures
  it with magic; she stays sharp and keeps writing. The Seafarers would ship her home to an asylum; the
  Islanders, whose priesthood takes in people who perceive what others can't, would look after her. The
  party can keep her on the island (she finishes the book at Namethare with Echedey, or at the Star Mother's
  temple) or fail to, and she's sent home (and leaves the party; her last letter comes in the epilogue).
  Her accuracy against spirits may quietly improve. Open: whether she has a husband (Routledge's was the one
  who had her committed).
- **Taboos and customs:**
  - **Seafarer:** nothing in a shell, and no shell jewellery or armor; and, as a grown woman, **no red meat**
    (the bull's flesh is for men; see lore.md, "The All-Seafarers").
  - **No alcohol:** *"A clear head is the only instrument I own."* (Routledge was raised a Quaker.) The one
    adult who turns down Yeray's wine.
  - Otherwise **eats what she's given** out of courtesy, asking first whose god she might offend.
  - **The collector's lesson:** she first wants to ship a First Sailor moai to Keftiu "where scholars can
    study it". Then she meets Tou, and learns the coral eyes she wanted are a person. After that she **never
    takes from a grave or a shrine** (in play: she won't equip grave goods), credits every informant, and
    honours "withheld at the priest's request" entries.
  - Salutes bull-men statues out of habit and treats beef as holy, like Thenmy. Won't sit while Thenmy
    stands, then remembers she disapproves of kings, and sits down very deliberately.

### Voices (writing guide)
How each party member talks, with sample lines. In real dialogue write `\N[n]` for names and the `\YP[...]`
codes for Yoon's pronouns (see the editor guide).

**Yoon:** curious, watchful, sweet, then suddenly uncanny. Short, literal sentences (still relearning
words); asks what others wouldn't; says the scary thing in the same tone as the nice thing.
- *"What's a 'tomorrow'? ...Oh. I want one."*
- (to a goat) *"You're warm. Everything alive is warm. I keep forgetting."*
- (after a battle) *"It stopped moving. Did it go where I went?"*
- (to Sib, sincerely) *"You're the first word I remembered."*

**Sib:** halting when nervous ("Um.", trailing off); fluent and precise about rites and inscriptions.
Tilts their head to listen on the good side.
- *"Um. Okay. So. The scroll says 'a bone of the body', and it doesn't say whose, so technically—no. No,
  that's bad. Forget I said that."*
- (reading, suddenly confident) *"It's a warning. 'The deep belongs to the Builders.' Third line's damaged.
  Probably something about dying horribly."*
- (lost) *"I'm sure it's left. ...Is it left? Lasa, which way is left?"*
- (flustered) *"You can't just—say things like that! I'm a priest! ...I'm a little priest!"*

**Lasa:** fast and excitable, repeats herself when emotional; treats humans like adorable, fragile pets.
- *"Ooh! Shiny! Mine! ...Ours. Mostly mine."*
- (teaching Ghost Yoon) *"Say 'rock'! Rock! No—that's 'Sib'. Sib's not a rock. Well. A little."*
- *"You humans break so easily. It's sweet. Don't do it, though."*
- (after the realisation) *"I'm supposed to be the one watching. I'm always supposed to be watching."*

**Pada:** clipped and plain, hunting metaphors; never says "I love you", but it's in everything.
- *"Eat. You're growing. ...You're doing something, anyway."*
- (tracking) *"Boar. Big one. Came through at dawn. Stay behind me."*
- (to Sib) *"You kept my child company when I couldn't. That's a debt, priest."*
- (to Yoon, quietly) *"Doesn't matter what you are. You came home."*

**Juda** (before the betrayal): gentle, measured, teacherly; answers questions with questions. Everything
reads differently on a replay.
- *"A body is only a vessel, Sib. What matters is what we pour into it."*
- (to Yoon) *"You're a remarkable child. I've rarely met anyone so... complete."*
- *"Rest. The mountain will still be there. It's very patient. So am I."*

**Dama:** short orders, dry humour, fishing words for everything.
- *"I don't care if you're a ghost, a demon or a talking squid. Someone killed a child. That's my business."*
- (to Parl) *"Run again and I'll net you like a mullet."*
- (battle start) *"Spread out. Make them pick a target."*
- (to Thenmy) *"Your soldiers bow to you. Mine argue. Mine are better."*

**Parl:** wry and self-mocking, with asides and footnotes; notices exits and price tags.
- *"In my defence, the pearls were just lying there. In a locked chest. On a guarded ship. Lying there."*
- (scribbling) *"Note: islanders have telegraphs. Note: empire very much does not. Note: nobody back home
  will believe this either."*
- (refusing shellfish) *"My god takes shells very seriously. I've stolen pearls and I'll still burn for an
  oyster."*
- (to Lasa) *"You take the shiny ones, I'll take the heavy ones. Partners."*

**Thenmy:** formal and courtly, long sentences, uses titles (as in his prologue narration).
- *"Priest Sib, I would be honoured if you'd correct my reading of this glyph. Again."*
- (stepping in front of a child) *"Behind me. That is not a request, it is a—fine. Beside me."*
- (foraging) *"This one is red, therefore poisonous. ...It's a berry you eat every morning? I see."*
- *"My father taught me that kings are chosen by the gods. Your people chose Dama by talking. I find I
  prefer the talking."*

**Falmi:** loud and enthusiastic; misunderstands idioms; describes feelings like weather or growth.
- *"I'll hit it! Can I hit it? I'm hitting it!"*
- *"Why do you all sleep at night? I just stand in the sun and I'm fine."*
- (hit hard) *"Ow! That's fine. It'll grow back. Everything grows back."*
- (to Tou) *"Your Majesty you look very—nice—wooden—I mean GOOD. Good wood. I'm going to go fight
  something."*

**Tou:** courtly and antique; issues decrees out of habit; slips between child and queen.
- *"We decree that this cave shall be less damp. ...Ah. It doesn't work outside the court, does it."*
- (to the Stonemen) *"Stand down. Your queen is here."*
- *"I fostered so many children. They always went home after a year or two. ...They did go home. Didn't
  they?"*
- (to Falmi, kindly) *"You may approach the throne, Sir Falmi. The throne is a rock today, but the
  principle stands."*

**Patience:** no voice; actions written as text, often the most emotional "line" in a scene.
- *Patience sniffs Ghost Yoon's hand that isn't there, and wags anyway.*
- *Patience growls at Juda. Nobody notices.*

### Party dynamics (draft: correct freely)

**Personalities**
- **Yoon:** shaped by the player, but curious and watchful at the core: relearning everything, language
  included, with a quiet fear of *what* they are. The listener of the group; others confide in them.
- **Sib:** devoted, anxious and bookish; a rule-follower who breaks the biggest rule (necromancy) out of
  love. Fragile body, stubborn spirit. After Juda, a child made the village's priest far too early.
- **Lasa:** impulsively kind (decided to help a ghost on sight), playful, a little light-fingered like the
  Children of the Caves, fascinated by humans. Politeness covers being an outsider. Darker arc: learning
  what the Children of the Caves really are.
- **Pada:** gruff, practical, protective; a grieving father who shows love by doing (and by giving his
  food to the children). Accepting a ghost child at face value is the biggest-hearted thing in the game.
- **Juda:** warm, wise, patient: the mentor everyone trusts. The mask.
- **Dama:** duty first: investigator, sworn spearwife, a leader who never asked to lead. Blunt, fair, slow
  to forgive, fiercely loyal once won.
- **Parl:** charming survivor, cynical and funny, writes everything down. Homesick and guiltier than he
  lets on. A pearl thief whose religion forbids anything from a shell.
- **Thenmy:** honourable, scholarly, earnest; privileged and slowly seeing what his empire is. Shields
  children on instinct; uncomfortable being the strongest person in the room.
- **Falmi:** four hundred years in the growing but newly born: earnest, energetic, literal-minded, with
  plant logic. Takes being heir very seriously. Hopelessly flustered around Tou.
- **Tou:** formal, polite, lonely. Centuries of role-playing a ruler made her genuinely good at it, and
  painfully sheltered about everything real. The most gracious person in the party, carrying the worst
  truth.
- **Patience:** a good dog.
- **Aday:** a tinkering, proud, rule-bound clan kid with a Seafarer grandfather: half of everything, and
  determined to prove it makes him more, not less.
- **Kathrout:** brisk, fearless, earnest; listens better than she talks. Learns the difference between
  studying a people and taking from them.

**Most friction**
1. **Dama and Thenmy:** the island's defender and the prince who invaded it, and they may marry. Enemies
   to grudging allies to more.
2. **Dama and Parl,** especially if his secret was kept (Option 2A): she hunted him, he lied to her.
3. **Tou and Lasa** (hidden): the Children of the Caves descend from the Crew children Tou's court
   sacrificed, with death warrants she signed unknowing. Lasa's people now cheerfully serve her. The truth
   is the most painful scene in the party: guilt, and maybe forgiveness.
4. **Tou and Sib** (hidden): Sib's necromancy comes from Juda's notes on First Sailor homunculus-making,
   Tou's civilization's crime, used to rebuild Yoon.
5. **Falmi and the Seafarers:** the forest's heir, and the Princess who burns forests.
6. **Mealtimes (comedy):** Yoon eats only land meat, Sib only birds, Dama only fish; three gods' worth of
   bickering at every Star Mother dinner. Parl refuses the shellfish; the children keep being turned away
   from the wine.

**Get along best**
- **Yoon and Sib:** the heart of the game.
- **Yoon and Lasa:** Yoon's first friend after death, who taught them to speak.
- **Falmi and Tou:** a crush, and everything in common: ancient and young at once, bodies that aren't
  flesh, raised as heirs by powerful elders.
- **Parl and Lasa:** two thieves with an eye for shiny things; Parl as the party's rogue uncle.
- **Thenmy and Sib:** scholar and priest: scripts, magic, Sib learning Seafarer writing.
- **Dama and Falmi:** a warrior mentoring the child tank.

**The kids' dynamic** (the comedy core):
- **Yoon:** the weird gremlin kid.
- **Sib:** the weird kid's nerdy best friend.
- **Lasa:** the bubbly, genki girl.
- **Tou:** the preppy rich girl (an ancient queen).
- **Falmi:** the jocky, high-energy kid.
A school-trip friend group, with a ghost, a fae, a doll queen and a forest prince in it.

**The royals** (Thenmy, Tou, Falmi; Dama as chieftess by consent)
- **Shaped by elders:** Tou a puppet of the Navigator Lords, Falmi grown four centuries to be heir,
  Thenmy a king's son in his sister's shadow. Dama was never raised to rule: divine kings versus rule
  by consent.
- **Not knowing what your realm does:** Tou's decrees killed children she thought she fostered; Thenmy
  is learning what tribute and conquest mean. The only two who understand that horror.
- **Tou as a primary source** for Thenmy's First Sailor scholarship: thrilling for him, awkward for her.
- **Protocol comedy:** Tou's ancient etiquette, Thenmy's courtliness, Falmi asking what manners are.
- **Real advice:** centuries of "solving" invented crises make Tou a surprisingly good adviser on
  governing the island after the war.
- **Lineage and death:** Tou can't die while her moai stands; Falmi has lived four hundred years;
  Thenmy is mortal and heir to a dying king. Tou laying her ancestors to rest opens these talks.
- **The island's future:** Tou's ancient claim, Thenmy's by conquest (or marriage), Dama's by the
  people. Who rules after the war?
- **Matchmaking:** Thenmy and Dama, Falmi and Tou; the royals teasing each other.

**Thenmy among the rebels** (a bronze prince working with "barbarians", most of them children, who in
most versions beat him)

How he takes it depends on how he was recruited:
- **Talked down:** he lost a debate, not a duel. Pride bruised but intact; he treats Yoon as a negotiator
  and the party as a strange embassy. The most open-minded version, curious from day one.
- **Defeated and spared:** a life spared is a debt (Mycenaean-style honour). He joins almost formally
  bound: stiff, proud, calling them by title ("Lady Chieftess", "Priest Sib"). The richest arc, from
  humiliation to respect to real loyalty.
- **The party surrendered to him:** in his mind he won, and sees himself as their guardian. The risk is
  condescension; his arc is learning they're allies, not wards. The first time a child saves his life
  should land hard.

In every version:
- **"Barbarians" with telegraphs.** Raised on the empire's prejudices (tattoos as slave marks), he finds
  batteries, telegraph lines and electric surgery made without bronze. Fascination and embarrassment: he
  takes notes, Sib teaches him Islander script in return for Seafarer writing, and Parl dryly points out
  that *his* notes said the same years ago.
- **Children who fight.** His deepest instinct is that adults shield children, so letting them fight feels
  shameful. He keeps stepping in front (the premier adult protector). The kids resent the coddling, Falmi
  most of all as the child tank; their rivalry over who stands at the front is comedy, and growth when he
  finally trusts a child to hold the line.
- **Protocol:** he bows to Tou, a queen older than his empire, and means it. Dama leads by consent, which
  baffles a prince raised on divine kingship; now he's the one who doesn't give orders.
- **Being slow:** a bronze glacier with the lowest Speed in the game, surrounded by fast children. A running
  gag.
- **A city boy:** he grew up in the Seafarer capital, a *huge* city by Bronze Age standards. Next to the
  Islanders, or even Parl (months alone in the highlands), he has **no wilderness skills at all**: he can't
  track, forage, fish, light a fire from scratch or tell a safe plant from a poisonous one. The children
  have to teach him. Comedy, and a quiet reversal of who the "primitive" ones are.
  - **In play:** he can only do **strength jobs** when gathering (felling trees, mining, stone, water), not
    knowledge ones (herbs, crops, digging roots, the shore, fishing). Actor note `<Gathers Only: ...>`.
  - **Teaching moments** unlock more, one scene each (script call `SmokingIsle.Gather.teach(11, 'fish')`):
    **Dama** teaches him to fish; **Sib or Falmi** which plants are safe (`herb`, `food`); **Yoon** digging
    roots with a stick (`dig`); **Lasa** or **Dama** the shore (`shore`). Each is a small scene of the
    children (and Dama) teaching the prince.
- **Meals:** no shellfish, beef as sacred food, and three Islander gods' worth of meat taboos at every Star
  Mother dinner. Shared meals are where he stops being a guest.

The two hardest beats:
1. **Learning what Juda did.** Juda killed Yoon partly to clear the way for Thenmy's own plan (the puppet
   chief, his sister's marriage). He never knew and would have refused, but his plan is part of why a
   child is dead, and that child stands next to him. It plays very differently depending on whether Yoon
   forgives him.
2. **Fighting his sister.** Still loyal to his father, he frames the war as saving his father's empire from
   what it's becoming, until he has to ask whether it was ever what he thought.

Where he ends up: treating the party as equals and friends. That feeds his endings (governing the island,
perhaps marrying Dama, or holding the capital when the old King dies): the first of his family to rule by
consent rather than destiny.

## Combat System

### Decisions
| Topic | Decision |
|-------|----------|
| Turn system | **FFX-style visible turn order** (no rounds): faster battlers get more turns, and costly actions delay your next turn |
| Attack command | Replaced by **Punch**, a weak blunt hit that ignores weapon ATK |
| Weapon skills | Equipped weapons unlock physical skills through **moves** (see below) |
| Fatigue | Replaces TP. Builds with most actions, **carries over between battles**, and causes **Weary** at 100 |
| Durability | **Condition tiers**: worn weapons turn into weaker items, and bronze never degrades |
| Weapon swapping | Mid-battle; costs the turn (Thenmy swaps for free) |
| Party swapping | Mid-battle, in the style of FFX: free, but adds a little Fatigue |

### Turn order
- There are no rounds. Each battler moves toward their next turn at a speed based on AGI
  (`AGI + 30` by default, which keeps big AGI gaps from getting out of hand). Whoever arrives
  first acts immediately: actors get their command on their own turn.
- A **Turn Order** window on the right shows the upcoming turns. While choosing an action, the
  ▸ marker previews where the current character will land afterwards.
- Actions cost 100 by default. `<Turn Cost: n>` makes an action quicker (below 100) or slower
  (above 100).
- `<Delay Target: n>` pushes the targets' next turn back on hit, which suits Dama's nets.
  Negative values pull an ally's turn closer.
- States, buffs and Fatigue regeneration tick on each battler's own turn.
- Enemy "Turn" conditions count that enemy's own turns. Troop event turns advance on every
  battler's turn.

### Weapon moves
Weapons carry **moves** (`<Moves: jab, thrust, sweep>`). A physical skill requires a move
(`<Requires Move: ...>`), so a character must both *know* the skill and have a weapon that *allows* it. The
weapon type (Dagger, Spear, Sword...) only decides who can equip it. Moves in use: jab, thrust, vital, slash,
chop, bludgeon, sweep, sword, metal, plus one per weapon family for its own arts: knife (daggers), brace
(spears, ritual spears), ward (staves), club (clubs, maces, hammers), hook (axes), paddle (paddle-swords),
offering (ritual knives), consecrate (ritual spears), pound (the two-handed basalt hammer and the
Dragonheart Greatsword), and the ranged moves sling, shoot, throw and net. The full skill list is under
Weapon skills above.

### Armor sets, charms and size rules
Built (`SI_Outfits.js`). Set bonuses are states in the **Set Bonuses** category of the States list
(`<Set Bonus: Tapa 2>`, `<Set Bonus: Tapa all>`); armor pieces carry `<Set: Tapa>`. The equip screen's
help box shows the set's progress ("Tapa set 2/4") and which bonuses are on. Size rules use armor types:
**Heavy Body**, **Heavy Head & Legs**, **Small Shield**, **Large Shield**, plus **Priestly** (Feather
set, Tattooist's Ink Pouch). Neck pieces (necklaces, pendants, amulets) use the Neck slot; rings and
gloves the hands; everything else in the list below is a Charm. The old placeholders were folded in:
Bark-Cloth Cloak → **Tapa Cape**, Cloth → **Tapa Wrap**, Ring → **Silver Ring**, Shield → **Wicker
Buckler**; the Hat and Shell Necklace were retired. Money is **cowries (CW)**.
Cave and lava-tube maps need `<Cave>` in their map note (as well as `<Indoors>`) for the cave charms
and the Lava-Tube set to work.
**Sets.** Clothing and armor pieces belong to sets. Wearing **2 pieces** of a set gives a small
bonus; the **whole set** gives the full bonus. A set with only two pieces gives its small bonus with
**one piece**. Charms never count toward sets. The equip screen shows the set and the count
("Tapa 3/4").

- Bonuses from different sets **stack** (a clothing set under an armor set is the normal outfit).
- **Evasion from sets is capped** (about +15% in total).
- **Talk bonuses stack too**: wearing two groups' gear (e.g. Boar Hide and Bronze) keeps both.
- The **Feather** set is priest-only (Sib, Juda, Yoon). It is **never crafted**: it's a small quest. You
  bring many feathers of each kind to a **grove priest** at a ritual site, who consecrates and makes it.
  Only the two grove priests may do it, so the game has **three sets**: Juda's own (he starts in it; it
  burns to ash with him in Act 3), one for **Sib** and one for **Yoon**.
- **Parts:** a set is made of parts, one per slot unless a piece names its own part. Pieces for the same
  part are substitutes, and several charms of one set still count once (the First Sailor Regalia names
  the eye and the pupil as separate parts). One piece can belong to two sets (Goat-Skin Moccasins).
- "First Sailor spells" are spells tagged `<School: first-sailor>` (none yet: Tou's spells are still to
  come); enemies tagged `first-sailor` (Stonemen, First Sailor ghosts) are still to come too.

### Voltaic techniques (Islander electricity)
`SI_Voltaic.js`. The **Voltaic Pile** (in its own **Battery** slot; every Islander starts with one) lets **Islanders** (Yoon, Sib, Pada, Dama, Juda) use
electrical techniques, under their own **Voltaic** skill type. Nobody else can wear a pile: it's
Islander technology, and the bronze-armed All-Seafarers don't have it.

- **Charge:** a pile holds **3 charge**. Techniques spend it, and it **carries over between battles**.
  Refill with **Acid Jars** (+2; acid from the hot springs) or a **battery tender** (full). The skill list
  shows "1 Ch (3)": spends 1, 3 left.
- **Electricity is physical Burn**, powered by the pile (Shock Power 14), reduced by Defense.
- **Water conducts:** electric techniques do **+50%** to a Wet or Sodden target (on top of Wet's own
  +15%), and **stun** a Sodden one (it loses its next turn). A **Wet user shocks themself** (10% of max HP).
- **Metal draws the current:** Live Wire does **×1.75** to enemies wearing metal (`<Metal>`: the
  bronze-armored invaders).

### Crafting
`SI_Crafting.js`. Crafting happens at **places**: you interact with a station.

**Stations**
- **Campfire:** in the wilds, often at Red Bowl, Sooty Bird or Star Mother idols. Must be **lit** with a
  **Fire-Starting Kit** (used up, once per session) and fed **fuel**. Not a rest point.
- **Earth oven:** villages, some ritual sites and resting areas. Always lit (unless the plot abandons the
  village), but still needs fuel, and gets more from it. Does every campfire recipe plus pottery, baking,
  drying and salting.
- **NPC crafters:** smith, tanner, weaver, tattooist, herbalist, electroplater. They take **materials plus
  cowries**, know all their own recipes, and sell recipes and portable equipment. If the plot abandons
  their village, they usually move away or die.
- Crafting yourself costs **materials only**. Party members don't matter.

**Fuel** adds charges to the fire for this session. **Nothing is made at a campfire until it's lit**
(making camp is the pause; the firelight is for working by), but only recipes that need heat use a
charge: carving, knapping, lashing and sewing don't. **Melting metal** also needs peat or coal to have gone in this session.
**Leaving the area** ends the session and unused charges are lost (the station warns you; a map note
`<Area: name>` lets a village's buildings share one area).

| Fuel | Campfire | Earth oven |
|---|---|---|
| Scrap Wood (also all driftwood) | 1 | 1 |
| Branch | 2 | 3 |
| Log | 3 | 4 |
| Peat (marshes) | 4 | 5 |
| Brown Coal (marshes, caves) | 5 | 7 |

**Recipes** are items (the Recipes category). You know a campfire or oven recipe while you carry it. Each is
bought **or** found, never both. **Carving is always known.** Every recipe takes **one of each** ingredient,
and only items in the bag are used, never equipped gear.

**Portable equipment** (key items): **Knapping Kit** (stone blades and heads; **Sib starts with one**),
**Fletching Kit** (arrows and bows; **Pada's**), **Backstrap Loom** (weaving; the weaver), **Needle &
Thread** (charms, clothing, nets and floats), **Cooking Pot** (boiling: syrups, teas, dyes, bone glue; the
herbalist), **Quern** (grinding flour, pigments, bonemeal and herb powders), **Crucible & Mold** (melting
your own ingots; the smith).

**Wood** comes from trees: cutting one gives a branch, and rarely a log, once per session (when overworld
harvesting exists). Each weapon type takes one or two woods, and the wood passes on its trait:

| Weapon | Woods | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spears | **Ash** (light: AGI +2) or **Pine** (resin: 10% less Fire damage); Heather for the plain Wooden Spear | A shaft must be long, straight and a little springy |
| Clubs, maces | **Ebony** (hard, heavy) or **Mahogany** (never splits) | A club *is* its wood |
| Axes, adzes, hammers | **Juniper** haft (durable) | The head does the work; the haft takes the shock |
| Paddle-swords | **Ebony** or **Mahogany** | The body must hold teeth or stone set along its edge |
| Staves | **Heather**, **Sacred Rain Tree** (glyphs come easier; never cut, only fallen or given), **Sandalwood** (Calm) | Three tiers for the casters |
| Bows | **Yew** or **Juniper** | The classic longbow wood, and the small dry-slope bow |
| Greatsword | **Dragonheart** only | Unique |

Laurel, Wax Myrtle (burns well), Honey Palm and Driftwood are firewood and building wood.

**Weapons**
- **Knives:** blade + **Sinew** + **Leather**. Stone blades need the Knapping Kit.
- **Spears:** carve a wooden spear (Ash, Pine or Heather), then + **Sinew** + a stone head. Wooden spears can
  be fire-hardened instead. Harpoons: Wooden Spear + Sinew + Bone or Shark Tooth.
- **Axes, adzes, hammers:** carve a Juniper Club, then + **Glue** + a stone head.
- **Clubs and maces:** carve an Ebony or Mahogany Club, then + **Glue** + coral studs, or clay (or
  potsherds) for a ceramic mace head at an earth oven. A Bare Club can be re-studded.
- **Paddle-swords:** carve an Ebony or Mahogany Paddle from a log, then + **Glue** + shark teeth, obsidian,
  jasper or chalcedony edging. On mahogany, worn-off edging leaves the plain Mahogany Paddle.
- **Staves:** carved from a branch; glyph staves are finished by a **tattooist**.
- **Swords** aren't crafted, except the **Dragonheart Greatsword**: a dragonheart branch, carved and
  fire-hardened.
- Sinew lashes the light weapons (knives, spears); glue sets the heavy ones (axes, maces, clubs, hammers,
  paddle-swords).
- Stone heads are not separate items: the stone goes straight into the weapon recipe.

**Metal.** You never forge weapons yourself.
- Copper Ore, Lead Ore and scrap **melt into ingots**: at the smith for a fee, or yourself with a
  **Crucible & Mold** (peat or coal on the fire). The island's silver is all in its lead (galena), so
  **lead sometimes also gives a Silver Ingot** (10%). Scrap copper-silver gives a copper and a silver ingot.
  There is no silver ore and no tin: players should never wonder why they can't make bronze.
- The **smith** makes copper weapons from ingots, and **straightens** bent copper, for a fee.
- The **electroplater** plates a copper weapon with a Silver Ingot into copper-silver, for a fee.
- Bronze, black bronze, grown coral and the mythic trophy weapons are never crafted.

### Party switching
- **Switch** battle command: lists reserve members (everyone beyond the four battle slots).
- **Free:** the incoming character takes the current character's place *and their turn*, and acts
  immediately. They gain a little Fatigue (default 1, scaled by TP Charge Rate) so switching
  can't be spammed.
- Knocked-out reserve members can't be switched in.
- `<No Switch>` on an actor keeps them out of the reserve list and stops them being switched out
  (for Patience).
- Guest characters only exist while the party has four or fewer members, so they never need
  switching rules.

### Resting
**The loop:** explore, harvest and fight until Fatigue gets high, then turn back or find a **resting
area** to craft, eat and sleep.

**Resting areas** (`SI_Idols.js`) have **some** of four things, each its own event:
| Thing | Common event | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Red Bowl Idol** | 6 | **Save.** The only place to save: the menu has no Save command. |
| **Sooty Bird Idol** | 7 | **Fast travel.** Touching one remembers it (event note `<Sooty Bird: Zonfahare>`); from any Sooty Bird idol you can fly to any other you've touched, landing on the tile below it. Flying takes a period of the day. **Not at night.** |
| **Star Mother Idol** | 3 | **Rest until dawn** or **share a meal.** |
| **Campfire / Earth Oven** | (crafting stations) | **Crafting.** |

Logical places: Yoon's family house, bothy-style shelters in the mountains, villages, hunters' camps.

**Resting** (Star Mother) sleeps until the next dawn: it restores **Fatigue and Mana** and clears lingering
ailments, but **not Health**. Anyone knocked out wakes with 1 Health. Everyone is hungry again.

**Health comes back by eating.** At a Star Mother idol you **share a meal**: pick one food for each party
member (Patience too); taboo foods can't be picked for someone who won't eat them. **Everything goes in one
pot**: the Health (and Mana) of all the food heals **every** party member, even those who didn't eat (Pada,
Juda); a Full Health food fills everyone. Each eater still gets their own food's lasting stat gain. One meal
per rest. **Meals can't be eaten from the menu.**

**Campfires are not resting areas** unless there's a Star Mother idol beside them. They're pit stops for
crafting along the way.

**The tired warning:** the first time anyone leaves a battle at 75 Fatigue or more, the game tells you to
find a Star Mother idol (once per rest; `SI_CombatCore` parameters *Tired Warning At* and *Tired Warning*).

**Resting skills** are physical (not spells, with a few exceptions) and live in the **Rest**
menu, so effects that block Physical skills (Weary, Mired) don't block resting.

| Skill | Who | Rule | Fatigue |
|-------|-----|------|---------|
| Catch Breath | Everyone | Only above 85 Fatigue; not while Weary | −15 to −20 |
| Heavy Panting | Everyone | Only above 85 Fatigue; works while Weary | −5 |
| Hunter's Repose | Pada | Only above 75; not while Weary; grants **Steady Aim** (+25% critical chance on his next ranged attack) | −20 to −25 |

Weary lifts as soon as Fatigue drops below 100, so letting a character reach 100 costs a turn of
Heavy Panting before Catch Breath. Since nothing works below 85 (75 for Pada), **Fatigue can't
be brought below about 60–70 without camping**.

**Physical conditioning:**
- **Falmi** sheds 1 Fatigue per turn in battle **outdoors**; **Tou** the same **indoors and in
  caves** (maps marked `<Indoors>`).
- **Sib's** physical skills and Punch cost **30% more** Fatigue (minor health complications);
  spells and other actions cost the normal amount.
- **Heavy bronze armor** adds Fatigue every turn (Bronze Helm +1, Bronze Cuirass +2).
  **Thenmy's Armor Training** (a passive skill) cancels this for him.

### Day and night
`SI_Time.js`. The day has four periods: **Dawn, Day, Dusk, Night**. The game starts by Day on day 1.
- **Time passes by walking** outdoors: 400 steps a period, and a battle counts as 40 steps. **Indoors and in
  caves** (`<Indoors>`, `<Cave>`) time stands still. Night turns to the Dawn of the next day.
- **Sleeping** at a resting point always wakes you at the next dawn (a new day).
- **Tints** outdoors: golden dawn, plain day, orange dusk, dark-blue night. `<No Time Tint>` opts a map out.
- **Darkness and light:** night is darker unless the party carries a light: a **Torch** (from the menu: 300
  steps), a **Myrtle Candle** (150 steps) or **Lamplight** (Shearwater Oil). At night, **gathering needs a
  light**, except night-only plants.
- **Night encounters:** map note `<Night Troops: 14, 15>` replaces the encounter list at night (ghosts,
  owls, Black Hounds, Sleep Demons).
- **Talk at night:** spirits start 10 friendlier, animals 10 warier.
- **For events:** variable 2 holds the period (0 dawn, 1 day, 2 dusk, 3 night), variable 3 the day, and
  switch 7 is ON at night, so event pages can use them as conditions (the river ghost, closed shops).
  Story events can jump the time: `SmokingIsle.Time.setPeriod(3)`.
- **The Sundial** (key item) tells the period and the day outdoors; it has no shadow at night and no sun
  indoors. A quest reward (like the Trachyte Quern).

### Darkness
Map notes (`SI_Time.js`): **`<Dark>`** for places that are always dark (caves, abandoned buildings) and
**`<Dark At Night>`** for places dark only at night (forests).
- Darkness is much darker than night unless the party carries a **light** (Torch, Myrtle Candle, Lamplight).
  A light burns down as you walk outdoors **or in the dark**, even underground where time stands still.
- **Without a light:** the party fights at **−15% hit** (enemies there are used to the dark), and nothing
  can be gathered.
- **Switch 8 "Can see"** is ON whenever the party can see. Use it as an event page condition for things
  only found by lamplight (hidden passages, glyphs, a ghost's footprints).

### Crawlways
`SI_Crawl.js`. A crawlway is an event with `<Crawlway: map, x, y>` (optionally a facing) in its note, calling
**Common Event 8: Crawlway**. **Only the children fit** (Yoon, Sib, Lasa, Tou, Falmi), plus Patience. With
adults in the party you choose: squeeze through with just the children, or stay together. The adults
**wait on the map they were on** and rejoin as soon as the party is back on it. Point crawlways at a
different map (a crawlway within one map would regroup at once). Story events can call
`SmokingIsle.Crawl.regroup()`.

### Lava-tube dungeons (design notes)
Real lava tubes (Cueva del Viento on Tenerife, Cueva de los Verdes and Jameos del Agua on Lanzarote, Ana Kai
Tangata on Rapa Nui) are long, winding tunnels left when a lava river drained under its own crust.
- **A spine with branches,** not a maze of rooms: one long route downhill toward the sea, side tubes for
  treasure and gathering. Overworld entrances (collapses, *jameos*) line up along the tube's path.
- **Skylights:** sunlit collapses as landmarks, resting areas (a Star Mother idol) and Rapa Nui-style
  **gardens** (sweet potato, taro, ti, bellflowers). Dark at night.
- **Darkness:** deep sections are `<Dark>`; Goblin's Gold and the Cave-Glow Stone glimmer as landmarks.
- **Stacked tubes and lava falls:** one-way drops to a lower level, and later a route back up as a shortcut.
- **Crawlways** only the children fit through; **Lasa flies** across broken benches; **Dama's** underwater
  breathing opens flooded sumps and the sea tunnel; adults clear rubble.
- **Hazards:** bad air near vents (Fatigue), steam, sharp rubbly floors, lavacicles.
- **Life:** blind crabs, cave rats, crickets, bats near entrances; the Children of the Caves' pranks and
  hoards; First Sailor ghosts deeper; the Destroyer's tubes deepest. Wart Fern, Cave Mushrooms, Goblin's
  Gold, Cave Pearls, Hot-Spring Sinter near vents.
- **Story places:** the Children of the Caves' village under a skylight garden; burial tubes (Islander
  mummies, First Sailor tombs, Tou's remains); a tube running through Tou's buried palace.
- **Variety along a long tube:** colour changes (black, rust-red, gold), skylights, lava falls, root curtains,
  pools. Tiles: recoloured cave tiles with raised wall benches.

### Gathering
`SI_Gather.js`. A gathering spot is a map event with `<Gather: Laurel>` in its note and one command, **Call
Common Event: Gather**. Copy and paste it for more of the same species. A second page with **Self Switch A**
as its condition can show a stump or a picked plant.
- **Once a day:** a spot gives once, then regrows at the next dawn (or after sleeping).
- **You pick who gathers.** Only party members who can do the job are offered; if nobody can, a message
  says what's needed ("This needs an adult with a metal axe equipped.").
  - **Adults only:** hard woods (Ebony, Mahogany) and mining. Only adults bring down **logs** and other
    heavy extras: a child with an axe gets the branch.
  - **Sacred plants** (Kava, Sandalwood, the Rain Tree's fallen branches, the Sacred Well): only **Yoon,
    Sib, Juda or Falmi** (actor note `<Gathers: sacred>`).
  - **Fishing:** **Dama or Parl** (`<Gathers: fish>`). **Cave plants** (Wart Fern): only **Lasa**
    (`<Gathers: cave>`).
  - **Children tire half as much** on light work: low plants, fruit, the shore, salt.
- **Tools, equipped by the gatherer:** cut plants need a **knife** (spurge, resin, bark, palm, reeds,
  gourds, sandalwood, limpets); trees an **axe** (hard woods a **metal axe**); ore a **pick**; fishing a
  **harpoon**. **By hand:** soft herbs, fruit, lentils, barley and orchil lichen. **Carried:** the **Knapping
  Kit** for loose stone, and a **Digging Stick** for roots (Yellowroot, Kava, Bracken, the Ti Root extra)
  and digging (clay, white clay, ochre, peat). Anyone can carve a Digging Stick from a branch at a
  campfire; it snaps into Scrap Wood now and then (5%). Water, salt and the shore's loose finds need
  nothing.
- **Picks:** the **Copper Pick** (smith: Copper Ingot, 60 CW) and **Bronze Pick** (Seafarer, never wears) are
  one-handed axes that pierce and crush. Copper wears into a **Bent Copper Pick** (the smith straightens
  it), which breaks into Scrap Copper and Scrap Wood.
- **Fatigue and wear:** each gather tires the gatherer (wood 3, ore 4, herbs, food, stone, digging, shore
  and fishing 2, water 0) and rolls wear on the tool used, like a swing in battle.
- **Falmi** (plants) doubles the chance of a plant's rare extra.
- **Night-only:** Moon Sorrel. The **Rain Tree** is never cut: its spot only rarely has a fallen branch.
- **Species:** woods (Laurel, Heather, Ash, Pine, Pine Resin, Juniper, Ebony, Mahogany, Yew, Dragon Tree,
  Sandalwood, Wax Myrtle, Honey Palm, Mulberry, Driftwood, Rain Tree); herbs (Bitterwood, Cliff Mint, Hill
  Thyme, Ironwort, Burnwort, Ti Plant, Yellowroot, Wart Fern, Moon Sorrel, Kava, Pennyroyal, Ephedra, Milo,
  Spurge, Orchil, Reed Bed, Gourd Vine); food (Barley, Lentils, Grapevine, Syrup Berries, Strawberry Tree,
  Bracken); stone (Obsidian, Chalcedony, Jasper, Basalt, Andesite, Scoria, Pumice, Olivine, Trachyte Cliff,
  Snowline Quartz, Hot Spring, Sulfur Vent); ore (Copper Vein, Lead Vein, Lodestone, Coal Seam); digging
  (Clay Pit, White Clay, Ochre, Salt Pan, Peat Bog); shore and water (Shore Rocks, Tide Pool, Seabird Cliff,
  Fishing Spot, Eel Pool, Spring, Sacred Well). The table is at the top of `SI_Gather.js`.
- **Animal parts** come from enemies, not spots.
- **Test room:** five sample spots along the bottom row (Laurel, Moon Sorrel, Obsidian, Clay Pit, Fishing
  Spot); the chest gives a Sundial, the picks and a Fishing Harpoon.

### Ranged weapons
Ranged weapons go in a separate **Ranged** equipment slot and can't be swapped mid-fight.

| Weapon | Skill | Damage | Uses per battle |
|--------|-------|--------|-----------------|
| Sling | Sling Stone | Crush | 5 |
| Bow | Loose Arrow | Pierce | 2–4 (per bow; Juniper Bow and Yew Longbow 3) |
| Javelin | Hurl Javelin | 30% Cut, 70% Pierce | 1 |
| Net | Cast Net | light Crush + **Entangled** (loses its next turn) | 1 |

- **Accurate:** ranged attacks ignore evasion. **High damage:** (unarmed ATK + the weapon's
  Ranged Power) × the skill's rate, so Yoon's sling outdamages their knife, and Pada's bow matches
  his Heavy Chop.
- **Dama's Fishing Harpoon** is a melee spear that also gives **2 javelin throws** per battle,
  leaving her Ranged slot free for a net.
- **Flying** enemies (and Lasa) dodge 75% of melee attacks, but not ranged or magic ones.
- **Wet bowstrings stretch:** a Wet or Sodden archer's bow loses 30% of its power, unless they wear a
  **Waxed Bowstring** or used **Myrtle Wax** this battle.
- **Ranged add-ons** (charms): Seabird-Fletched Arrows (+1 arrow), Reed Arrows (+2 arrows, bow power −3),
  **Net Floats** (nets also soak: Wet). The **Palm-Fibre Net** gives two net casts a battle.

Who can use which ranged weapons follows the character notes: Yoon, Sib, Juda, Parl and Tou use
slings; Pada and Parl bows; Dama, Falmi and Thenmy javelins; Dama nets.

### Weapon swapping and wear
- **Swap** battle command: lists carried weapons the character can equip. Swapping uses the turn
  (the "Swap Weapon" action; give it a `<Turn Cost>` to make it quicker or slower).
- `<Free Swap>` (Thenmy, set on his actor): swap once per turn without using it, then act.
- **Wear tiers:** `<Degrades To: id>` and `<Wear Chance: %>` on weapons and armor. A worn item
  turns into the next tier, and `<Degrades To: 0>` means it breaks.
- **What's left when it breaks depends on the material:**
  - **Stone, bone and wood** disappear.
  - **Copper** (metal) leaves an unusable broken item in the inventory that can be **repaired or
    sold**: `<Breaks Into: item id>`.
  - Weapons wear when a skill uses one of their moves. Punch never wears weapons.
  - Armor wears when its wearer takes physical damage.
  - Items without the tags (bronze, unique gear) never wear.
  - Repairs are handled by events (smiths, shops).

| Weapon | Wears into | Chance per use |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Material | Wears into | Chance per use (then the worn tier) |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| Shell | Chipped, then breaks | 6%, then 9% |
| Obsidian (brittle) | Chipped/Cracked, then breaks | 4–5%, then 6–8% |
| Tusk, shark tooth | Cracked/Gap-Toothed, then breaks | 4%, then 6% |
| Basalt | Chipped, then breaks | 3%, then 5% |
| Copper | Bent, then breaks into **Scrap Copper** (kept) | 2%, then 3% |
| Copper-silver | Bent, then breaks into **Scrap Copper-Silver** (kept) | 1%, then 2% |
| Dragonheart wood | Cracked, then breaks | 1%, then 2% |
| Coral studs | Bare Club (raw wood); the studs leave **Bonemeal** | 6% |
| Ceramic | Shatters (breaks; that swing hits ×1.5) | 10% |
| Raw wood (ebony, heather, rain-tree, juniper, ash, pine) | Splits and breaks | 2% |
| Mahogany-bodied edging and heads | Fall off, leaving the plain Mahogany Paddle or Club | as the edging |

**Nothing that wears breaks into nothing** (`<Breaks Into: a, b>`): stone leaves **Stone Caltrops**; shell,
teeth, tusk and bone leave **Bonemeal**; ceramic leaves **Potsherds**; copper leaves **Scrap**; and anything
with a haft or shaft also leaves **Scrap Wood**. Repairs follow how a weapon was made: **Sinew Binding** for
sinew-lashed weapons (knives, spears, harpoons), **Glue** for glued ones (axes, hammers, clubs, maces,

| Fire-hardened wood, bronze, black bronze | never wear | — |
| Gold-plated copper | half as often as copper | the plating cracks off: bent copper |

These are placeholder examples; real wear rates need playtesting.

### Fatigue
- Each skill adds its "TP Gain" value as Fatigue **once per use** (MV's default adds it once per
  target hit). The `<Fatigue: n>` note tag overrides this, and negative values are used for Rest.
- The "TP Charge Rate" trait scales how fast a character tires, which is how Sib "gets tired
  quickly".
- At 100 Fatigue, a character becomes **Weary**: Magic, Physical and Punch are sealed, leaving
  Guard, items and Rest skills. Weary ends when Fatigue drops below 50.
- Fatigue persists after battle. "Recover All" (an inn or event) resets it.
- Taking damage does not add Fatigue (can be switched on in the plugin settings).
- Enemies start each battle at 0. An enemy that can become Weary needs a Rest skill in its
  action list, or it will do nothing once it is Weary.

### Insight (leveling)
The level is called **Insight**: how well a character understands the world. It is gained only
by talking, and it makes characters **more resilient and perceptive, never more dangerous**.
Damage comes from weapons, Yoon's body parts and charms. Skills come from sidequests,
relationships, inscriptions and the story.

**Shown as a number** ("Insight 7").

**Every character has their own Insight**, used for:
- **Stats** (per-class growth curves). Insight affects **only**:
  - **Spirit** (MDF, magic defense): you understand magic and spirits, so they hurt you less
  - **Wits** (LUK): your ailments land, and theirs don't
  - **Evasion**: you read what a creature is about to do
  - **Mental resistance** (mental states and the Mind element)
  - **Critical-hit chance**: you see where it is weak

  Everything else (HP, MP, ATK, DEF, MAT, AGI) is unaffected by Insight.
- **HP and MP** grow from:
  - **Quest milestones** (Yoon's body parts count as these)
  - **Armor and charms**
  - **Consumable items** that permanently raise them
- **Their specialty conversations**, where their Insight unlocks extra options, better odds or
  instant peaceful outcomes.

| Character | Specialty |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Sib | Spirits, forest beings, ritual encounters |
| Pada | Animals |
| Juda | Priestly and religious encounters |
| Dama | Islanders, militia, sea creatures |
| Parl | All-Seafarer common soldiers, deserters, smugglers |
| Thenmy | All-Seafarer officers and nobility |
| Tou | First Sailor beings: Stonemen, Hags, Owls of Ill Omen, ancient ghosts |
| Falmi | Plants, forest fae, the Lady of the Woods' court |
| Lasa | Children of the Caves, fae |
| Patience | Dogs and animals (passive: helps whoever is speaking) |
| Yoon | No specialty: a generalist |

**Yoon's Insight drives everything else:**
1. **Perception:** reveals enemy HP, then weaknesses, then mood during conversations. It also
   lengthens the Turn Order window, and at high Insight may reveal enemies' next actions.
2. **Comprehension:** in Act 1, NPC dialogue is partly garbled and becomes readable as Insight
   rises. Later this gives partial understanding of All-Seafarer and First Sailor text (Parl, Tou
   and Falmi still give full readings).
3. **Reading people:** extra dialogue options, catching contradictions, spotting lies, and
   noticing Juda's tells. Insight checks *add* understanding but never lock out anything the plot
   needs.
4. **The ghost-or-demon question:** high Insight reveals ambiguous hints without answering it.

**Caps:** each character's Insight cap is roughly based on **how magical they are**. Yoon's is
high; most others are low, so each of their levels matters. A first-pass ordering (numbers to be
decided):

| Tier | Characters |
|------|------------|
| Highest | Yoon, Tou, Sib, Juda |
| High | Falmi, Lasa |
| Medium | Thenmy (paladin magic), Dama (water magic) |
| Low | Pada, Parl |
| Fixed at 1 | Patience |

**Starting balance** (placeholder numbers, to be tuned in playtesting):

| Per Insight point above 1 | Amount |
|---------------------------|--------|
| Spirit (MDF) | +1 (class curve) |
| Wits (LUK) | +1 (class curve) |
| Evasion | +0.5% |
| Mental states (Rage, Confusion, Fascination, Sleep) and Mind element | land 2% less often |
| Critical-hit chance | +1% |
| Talk (mood changes) | +3% stronger (`SI_Talk`'s Insight Bonus) |

| Insight cap | Characters |
|-------------|------------|
| 30 | Yoon (Ghost and Human) |
| 15 | Sib, Juda, Tou |
| 12 | Lasa, Falmi |
| 8 | Dama, Thenmy |
| 5 | Pada, Parl |
| 1 | Patience |

For example, Sib at Insight 15 has +14% evasion, mental effects land 28% less often, and Fatigue
builds 14% slower. Yoon at 30 reaches +14.5% evasion, 58% and 29%. All other stats are flat across
Insight.

**Gaining Insight:**
- **In battle:** whoever's turn it is does the talking, so swapping the right specialist in
  becomes a tactic. Suggested split: the speaker earns full EXP, active party members a share,
  and benched members a smaller share.
- **Outside battle** (interrogations, NPCs, story and relationship scenes): **Yoon only**.
- **Avoiding grinding:** the first conversation with a new species, spirit or person gives full
  EXP, and repeats give little. Harder conversations give more.
- **Ghost and Human Yoon** (actors 1 and 2) share one Insight value.
- **Late joiners** (Dama, Thenmy, Falmi, Tou) need a sensible starting Insight. To be decided.

### Talk
**Model:** a hybrid. A **mood meter** runs the mechanics, and enemies can carry short written
lines plus optional scripted moments (a common event with choices) for important encounters.

**Flow**
- **Talk** is a main battle command and **takes the speaker's turn** (plus a little Fatigue).
- It opens a list of **approaches** the speaker can use:

| Approach | Who can use it | Idea |
|----------|----------------|------|
| Soothe | Everyone | General calming |
| Offer | Everyone | Give an item (food, offerings); enemies like or hate specific items |
| Ask | Everyone | Seek information once the enemy is calm enough |
| Rite | Sib, Juda, Tou, Falmi | Spirits respond to rites, often only the right one |
| Threaten | Adults (Pada, Juda, Dama, Parl, Thenmy) | Some beings respect force, others panic or rage |
| Plead | Children (Yoon, Sib, Lasa, Tou, Falmi) | The inverse of Threaten: a child's plea is hard to ignore |
| Stalk | Pada, Parl, Falmi | Animals only: works toward **Hunted** instead of calm |

- **Mood** runs from **Enraged** through **Hostile**, **Wary** and **Calm** to **Pacified**, and is
  only ever shown **as words**, never as a bar or number. Each
  enemy type reacts to each approach differently (set by note tags, with defaults per talk type:
  animal, spirit or sapient).
- **The speaker matters:** their specialty and Insight strengthen their approaches, and
  Patience passively boosts animal conversations.
- **Yoon's Insight decides what the player can see:** first the enemy's mood, then which
  approaches it likes, then its tells.
- **Enraged:** a badly handled enemy gets stronger and stops listening for a while.
- **Hitting an enemy angers it:** every hit that does HP damage (Patience's bites included) costs
  10 mood (`<Talk Hit: -20>` per enemy). At 0 it becomes Enraged. Fighting and talking work
  against each other.

**Reactions, results and questions** (mostly predictable; smart enemies can be the capricious exceptions)
- **Every approach gets its own reaction** from each enemy: one for when it works, one for when it
  backfires, with random variants (`<Talk Soothe+: ... | ...>`, `<Talk Offer-: ...>`). Enemies without
  custom lines use defaults for their talk type.
- **Weighted results:** an approach usually does its normal mood change, but can have twists
  (`<Talk Offer Results: 85 normal | 15 leave "...">`): **leave** (takes it and goes, no reward),
  **flee**, **lash** (hits the speaker), **gift**, **demand** (wants another item: a yes/no prompt),
  **info**, **event**. Bad results are twice as likely while it's Hostile and half as likely when the
  speaker's specialty matches.
- **What Yoon can read (Insight):** choosing a Talk target shows a hint box. At Insight 2 it shows the
  enemy's mood; at 4, which approaches it responds to and which offend it; and from 3 up, its **tells**
  (`<Talk Tell: It keeps sniffing toward your bag.>`, `<Talk Tell 5: ...>` for deeper ones). At the
  start of a battle Yoon points out any tell they can read ("Yoon notices: ...").
- **Enemies ask questions** (40% chance after being talked to, each once per battle) through a choice
  window. Answers change mood, get a reply, and can have results (leave, flee, lash, gift, steal).
  **Some answers only appear when a particular character is there** (Pada to the Boar-Men, Parl to
  the Marines, Lasa to the Children of the Caves, Falmi to the imps).

**Enemy moods and behaviour**
- Enemies can **start calmer** (`<Talk Mood: Wary>` or a number); the default is Hostile (25).
- **Calm enemies don't attack.** They only use non-harmful actions, or wait.
- **Enemies can talk too:** a talk skill runs a common event, written like any event (choices,
  switches, Abort Battle). Enemy skills can require a mood (`<Talk Min Mood: 35>`).
  - **Offer Passage** (spirits, once Wary): leave peacefully, which ends the battle, or stay.
  - **Demand Surrender** (soldiers): surrender, which turns on the "Surrendered" switch and ends
    the battle, or refuse. This could also be the "surrender to Thenmy" recruitment path.
- **Instant success:** some enemies yield at once to a specific speaker (`<Talk Instant: 9>`).
  **Stonemen surrender immediately when Tou talks to them.**

**Outcomes** (each enemy defines which it gives when Pacified):
- **Pacify:** it leaves the battle.
- **Information:** a clue, a weakness or lore. It may set a switch for the mystery.
- **Items:** it gives something, then leaves.
- **Temporary help:** it heals HP, restores MP, **buffs the party**, or (in certain situations)
  **debuffs the remaining enemies**, then leaves.
- **Hunted** (from Stalk, animals only): the animal's actions are disabled for **3 turns**.
  Suitable characters get a **finishing move that instantly kills a Hunted animal**.

**Battle end:** if every enemy is pacified, hunted down or gone, the battle is won.

**Insight EXP**
- Comes from successful conversations, using the enemy's EXP value in the database. Battle
  victories give no EXP.
- The speaker earns the full amount, active party members a share, and reserve members a
  smaller share.
- The first success with each enemy type gives full EXP; repeats give much less.

### The mystery (design)
The player can't solve the murder directly. **Evidence collected before the mountain** decides how
the confrontation goes: the more evidence, **the sooner Dama arrives** after Juda burns Pada to
death, the **better armed** the villagers with her are, and the **fewer NPCs Juda kills** before
"dying". See lore.md, "The murder", for what happened and the draft clue list.

### Difficulty, encounters and defeat (design)
- **Difficulty escalates with the story:** forgiving early (unless you wander into optional
  "sting" areas), tense later.
- **Encounters:** about 80% are **visible enemies** on the map; random encounters only in certain
  caves and dark areas.
- **Fatigue is an expedition resource.** Abilities raise it **very slowly**, because it carries
  over between fights and also comes from strenuous overworld actions:
  - Scrabbling up cliffs, mining, swimming, squeezing through tight tunnels, and so on.
  - Normal walking adds nothing, except in a handful of late-game areas (underwater, certain
    lava tubes, the volcano crater, the volcanic desert).
  - **Recovery:** camping at set spots, shelters and the village.
  - **The Ghost has no Fatigue at all** (`<No Fatigue>` on the Ghost class). Fatigue arrives
    when Yoon gets a body.
- **Losing a fight:**
  - **Early game** (Ghost, Sib and Lasa): Sib runs back to their house.
  - **Acts 2–3** (with Pada, then Juda): the adult carries the children back to the nearest save
    point.
  - **Cost of being rescued:** defeated or pacified enemies come back, and Pada scolds you (not
    strictly a punishment).
  - **After Act 3:** a hard Game Over, and the game warns the player when this starts.

### Damage types
**Six damage types:** Physical, Magical, Cut, Pierce, Crush, Burn. Physical Burn covers
electricity, poison and acid.

An attack has a **damage profile**: one or more parts, each a share of the damage with a type,
landing physically (DEF, physical damage rate) or magically (MDF, magical damage rate). Each part
is multiplied by the target's resistance to its type, so a mixed attack is partly resisted rather
than all-or-nothing. Rare enchanted items and late-game weapon skills can mix physical and magical
parts.

| Attack | Profile |
|--------|---------|
| Punch, Bash | Crush |
| Quick Jab | Pierce |
| Heavy Chop | 70% Cut, 30% Crush |
| Bite (Patience) | 70% Pierce, 30% Crush |
| Enemy Strike | generic Physical |
| Ahi (fire) | magical Burn |
| Rari (water: jets, ice spikes) | magical 70% Pierce, 30% Crush |
| Hagu (wind) | magical 80% Cut, 20% Crush |
| Keri (earth) | magical 80% Crush, 20% Pierce |

**Magic sources** (Fire, Water, Air, Earth, Mind) are separate from damage types: they record
where magic comes from, so a fire spirit can ignore fire magic yet still be hurt by electric
Burn. Tou is weak to all Burn damage (her fire weakness is no longer counted twice).

Note tags: `<Damage: cut 70, crush 30>`, `<Damage: physical pierce 60, magical burn 40>`,
`<Source: fire>` (`SI_DamageTypes.js`).

### Elemental states
The four magic sources form a cycle. As a general rule (exceptions exist, e.g. volcanic acid pot
items that do physical Burn damage *and* inflict Sodden), an elemental hit does, in order:

| Element | 1. Removes (either tier) → leaves | 2. Upgrades | 3. Inflicts |
|---------|-----------------------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Fire | Burdened/Mired → **Cracked** | Disoriented → Feverish | Smoldering |
| Water | Smoldering/Enflamed → **Steam** | Burdened → Mired | Wet |
| Wind | Wet/Sodden → **Chilled** | Smoldering → Enflamed | Disoriented |
| Earth | Disoriented/Feverish → **Grounded** | Wet → Sodden | Burdened |

**Removing a second-tier state gives the attacker an extra turn** (like SMT's Press Turn), and
with the turn order they act again immediately.

| State | Effect (placeholder numbers) | Turns |
|-------|------------------------------|-------|
| Smoldering | Lose 5% HP per turn | 3 |
| Enflamed | Lose 12% HP per turn; loses every other turn | 3 |
| Wet | Takes 15% more physical damage | 3 |
| Sodden | Takes 35% more physical damage; can't use Magic | 3 |
| Burdened | AGI −20% | 3 |
| Mired | AGI −45%; can't use Physical skills or Punch | 3 |
| Disoriented | Hit −15% | 3 |
| Feverish | Hit −35%; 30% chance per turn to lash out at a random target (enemies with their own attack) | 3 |
| Cracked | Takes 60% more physical damage (the stone shell shatters) | 1 |
| Steam | Hit −50% (vision obscured) | 1 |
| Chilled | AGI −60% (muscles seize) | 1 |
| Grounded | ATK −50% (momentum lost) | 1 |

Elemental hits always apply their effects for now (one setting if it proves too swingy).
Damage over time (burning, Bleed) **can kill**, party members included. "1 turn" states last until the
end of the target's next own turn. Feverish
counts as a mental state for Insight's mental resistance. Exceptions: `<Elemental: water>`,
`<No Elemental States>`, `<Inflict: sodden>` (`SI_ElementalStates.js`).

**The Lady of the Woods: two aspects, two true forms.** Both fights start as her current battle, with
opposite talk rules, and at half Health she shifts (troop event; her mood carries over):
- **Lowland** (rainforest): gregarious and temperamental. Food, plain manners and pleading work; threats
  are a disaster. Shifts into **the Sacred Rain Pig** (after the Guanche Aranfaybo): wordless and holy,

- **Alpine** (high pines): polite but cold. Courtesy works (Ask, Rite); **gifts insult her**; tears don't
  move her; threats are unforgivable. Shifts into **the Stern Centaur**, a judge with a spear: only formal
  questions and rites reach her; Spear of the Pines, Hoofbeat, Frost of the Heights, and her glyph Huhuti.
- Simulated: both about 95% wins for the Act 2 party fighting (about 60% Health lost); talking her down

**How they play:**
- **Shearwaters** are night encounters (put their troop in a coast map's `<Night Troops>`); Crying Chorus
  can Panic the whole party. Their tells hint at the Master of Deception's crying-child trick.
- **Gannets** dive-bomb (Plunge Dive); **Hawks** use the Golden Eagle's Talon Dive.
- **Jellyfish** sting whoever hits them with a weapon: enemy note `<Stings: Venom 30>` (`SI_BattleTraits`).
  Slings, spells and talking avoid it.
- **The Spider Crab** leads the shore crabs: at 70% Health two more scuttle in (troop event).
- **Wild Bees** guard Bee Rocks. They can't be talked to; a lit **Torch** (Torchlight on anyone) scatters
  them at the start of a turn (troop event), and fire hurts them double. Put the troop on the Bee Rock.
- **The Laurel Pigeon** is the gentle fight that teaches Talk: starts friendly, Soothe and seeds work
  fast, and it may fly off after turn 2.
- **Water Snakes** and **Snow-line Hares** flee a threat (`<Talk Flees: threaten>`); hares may also bolt.
- **The Heron** is the Crane's twin (the same sprite, recoloured) and genuinely a heron.
- **The Aurochs Calf** is harmless and friendly, but the moment it's hurt the herd bull charges in
  (troop event). The **Aurochs** drops Cowhide and Ox Horn.
- **The Ridden Aurochs** works like the Hunched: shrugs off Venom and Bleed, weak to fire, and a rite
  frees the carcass.

### Database layout
Every list is grouped into categories. Each category starts with a header entry
(`------- Name -------`, never used in game) on an ID ending in 1 (except right after the fixed entries), followed by its entries and
at least 25 blank slots for new things of that kind. Add new entries in the blank slots of their
category. RPG Maker's fixed entries stay first: Skill 1 (Punch, the Attack command), Skill 2
(Guard) and State 1 (Knockout). Plugin parameters and tests refer to these IDs, so moving an
entry later means updating those references too (the reorganization used a script for this).
